## Context
Review-sweep-D (M1-NAR-08 Review log) found that the run after the wedding followed the inspiring game in order:
1. a Warden revealed as a revenant in the wedding fight;
2. flight beneath the holy city through a drowned under-passage, to an undead or bound-dead construct boss;
3. a journey straight on to the world's edge, where the founding truth of the ritual is learned;
4. the high cleric asking the heroine to "sing *him* in", that is, to make him the calamity's power.

That chain is the inspiring game's holy-city wedding, the condemned sent beneath the city through a water labyrinth with an undead boss, the truth at the ruined city, and the cleric who wants to become the calamity.

SPEC §3.5 requires only four things in Act II: the wedding trap, the heretic declaration, Kerrid's reveal, and learning at the Drowned Choir what the Hush really does. SPEC §3.4 fixes Sefir's *motive*: to become the Undertow's eternal heart. It doesn't fix what he *asks* of Asha.

## Options
1. **A public trial, then exile to sea** (one of the Lead's examples). Rejected. A trial in the holy city before a condemned journey is itself one of the inspiring game's beats.
2. **Imprisonment in the Lamp Archive, then rescue by an ally** (the Lead's other example). Partly taken. The Archive becomes the place of the truth, *before* the wedding. The ally's rescue becomes the escape itself.
3. **Escape up and out, across the water; the truth on paper, earlier; a temptation to go home (chosen).** Details below.

## Decision
**(b) The founding truth moves to Ch13, in the Lamp Archive, before the wedding.** As a show of peace, Sefir gives Liss the run of the Order's library of Lit Age records for the three days before the wedding. With Ysolde she finds:
- the founders' lamp-ledgers, proving the Lamplighters were binders who bound themselves and the storm together;
- the Cantor roll with its hidden *kept / spent* column. Corran is listed as *kept, Sanctum*, with *to be spent at the next Calm* in the margin. Ysolde does the maths on Corran here, not in Ch15.
- the same few hands signing across four hundred years. This is the revenant truth, laid out as paperwork, and nobody names it yet.

The Cistern and the Underreach stay in the lore as what the ledgers describe, but **no zone goes under the city**. The founders' records also leave Lowlight (Ch11). There, Liss now learns that Rhen is Dace's son from a Saltwright housing-mark, so no truth of the ritual lands in the cold Ice sanctum. The Choir keeps only what SPEC §3.5 gives it: Kesh shows *what the Hush does*, that the singer becomes the next heart.

**(a) The escape goes out across the water, never under the city (Ch14).** Merel Dask, the bride, widowed for a minute, cuts her bridal barge loose and poles the party out onto the lagoon where no wake is permitted. The first ripples in four hundred years spread under the whole city's eyes. Merel stays to drop the water-gate behind them, and the party watches her taken. That is the ally's cost, seen.
- **Boss:** Warden Malcor Venn on the pursuit barge, living-seeming. **His revenant reveal is cut from the wedding.** The truth about the Conclave reaches the player through the Archive ledgers. Revenants are first fought openly in Ch19.

**Ch15 is "Unlamped": the open sea, then Hollowmere.** The unlamped bridal barge drifts at night.
- **Boss:** *the Wakefin*, an Undertow-spawn that followed the wake. It is a creature of the storm, not an undead construct, and it replaces the Cistern Keeper.
- **Kerrid's reveal** (below).
- Hollowmere answers Liss's current-song at dawn. Tobin's father learns of his son's death, Toma's faith breaks, and Toma makes his confession. Tobin's mourning moves here from Ch17.
- Ch16 begins from a Hollowmere ship that waits at the Pale Rim. The journey to the world's edge is therefore a chosen voyage after a chapter of grief among allies, not a flight straight on from an escape.

**(c) Sefir's temptation is to go home (Ch16).**
- **The offer:** "Leave the Eighth Verse in the stones. Go home to Haluna. Live. No Cantor will ever be named again. I will see to the storm myself." It is the Long Stillness offered as a gift: nobody pays and nothing ends.
- **Why it fits our Choir mythology:** Kesh hid the Eighth Verse, the only thing that can undo him, in those stones, and "I will see to it" hides that he means to take the heart himself.
- **What happens:** Ysolde asks who the storm takes then, and he doesn't answer. Asha refuses. He never asks anyone to sing him in. In Ch21 he reaches in and takes the heart (H05), which keeps SPEC §3.4's motive.

**Kerrid's reveal is not staged like the inspiring game's wedding revenant reveal.** The old version had the Stillguard perform a rite (the Stilling) on him during the wedding escape, so the dead man was exposed by a priestly rite at the wedding. That was an echo of the inspiring game, where the heroine performs the rite on the undead cleric at the wedding. The rite is now gone from the wedding. Kerrid's reveal happens at sea in Ch15:
- held dead from an old Rising drift toward him the way held light drifts to the sea, and a few of his motes start to leave his hands;
- he chooses to tell them himself, and nobody attacks or performs anything on him;
- Toma's Ch12 clue (the unfogged breath) pays off in two lines.

It is a confession among friends. It is not a reveal in a fight, at a wedding, or at the end of the pilgrimage.
