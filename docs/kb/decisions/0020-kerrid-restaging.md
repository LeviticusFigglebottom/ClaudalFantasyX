## Context
Review-sweep-E (M1-NAR-09 and M1-NAR-08 Review logs) found that Kerrid's backstory follows the inspiring game's dead mentor almost point for point. ADR 0016 and 0017 had checked only his introduction, costume props and the staging of his confession:
1. he guarded the last sacrificial pilgrim alongside the hero's father;
2. at the ritual's final site he understood too late, drew steel on the man responsible, and was killed;
3. two promises keep him walking: to watch over the heroine, and to bring up the hero;
4. his face cues: a covered lower face, one ruined eye, a facial scar;
5. he returns to the place he died (Ch16), and he is the first to depart in the finale.

**SPEC fixes only this (§3.4, §3.5):** silent, scarred, ronin-like; knew both Rhen's and Asha's parents; secretly one of the dead staying among the living; revealed in Act II; released in the Great Unbinding. Everything listed above was our choice.

The sweep also left smaller items:
- **NAR-09 S1:** the Ch7 shape (the newcomer fills a gap on a small island side) is not in 0017's table, and the outcome isn't pinned.
- **NAR-09 S2:** Sefir's "I knelt beside your mother" (Ch6) is not in the table.
- **NAR-09 S3:** the Prologue line is the one near-verbatim text echo of the inspiring game's opening line, and 0017 accepted it. Since then the human has approved replacing it (relayed by the Lead, 2026-10-23).
- **NAR-09 S4:** act_outline still says "the Iskarra fire".
- **NAR-08 S2:** Liss is the chief's daughter with an heir brother, and the ship comes from her people's haven.
- **NAR-08 S3 and nits:** E6 blocking, the Stay-branch hand, and E5's 0.04 sea-glow reading the same as E6's 0.0.

## Options (Kerrid)
1. **Keep him as Imra's guardian, but make him die in the storm after the Choir, pulling someone out** (the sweep's first suggestion). Rejected. It still has him guarding the last pilgrim with the hero's father, and it keeps his death at the end of her pilgrimage.
2. **Keep the guardian role and give him one promise, to Imra, about Asha only.** Rejected. It is half of the same two-promise structure, and "watch over the heroine for her parent" is the half that is most recognisable.
3. **He was never a guardian. He met Imra and Dace on one night at Corrow, held the Fisher Stair for them, and died at its foot. What keeps him is an unfinished watch, not a promise to anyone (chosen).** It uses a place and a rule the player already knows from Ch8 (ADR 0017 (c)), and it gives him a reason to leave last at the end.

## Decision

### (a) How Kerrid died, and why
- **1001 AL, during Imra's pilgrimage, the Undertow came for Corrow.** Kerrid was the Stillguard captain of the wall. Imra and her guardian Dace Sallow were in Corrow for the Coilspire trial and had been his guests for a week. He and Dace drank together. He knew them both for eight days.
- When the storm came, **Imra went down into the low town against her escort's orders to bring people up**, because she could hear the drowning. Dace went after her. Kerrid held the Fisher Stair's wall-gate open past the hour the rule allows, went down the stair himself, and got them both up.
- **He was the last man on the stair, holding the hand-line for the last climbers, when the sea came over it.** The line ran through his hands and burned them to the bone, and he held it anyway. Dace reached back down for him and missed. The sea took Kerrid off the bottom step.
- He woke at the next low tide at the foot of the stair, among the drowned, as one of the unrisen. Corrow had already lit his name. As a captain he had performed the Stilling twice, so he knew what the Order would do with him, and he was gone before anyone saw him.
- **He did not die at the Choir, and he never fought Sefir.** He has never been to the Pale Rim. Imra went on, sang, and was spent. Dace, who had already lost one man off a stair, went into the storm after her.
- *Why it serves the pillars:* his death is the Corrow defence the player has already lived through in Ch8, done eleven years earlier and also failing at the margin. Its sting is pillar 6. He saved Imra's life so that she could walk on to the Choir and be spent, and he was proud of it until Ch16.

### (b) What keeps him walking: a watch, not a promise
- The Stillguard stair-law at Corrow is **"First down the stair, last up it."** A captain doesn't come off the wall while anyone is still below. Kerrid never came off. His will is fixed on a watch that never ended, not on a promise to any person.
- For eleven years he has walked the coasts. Wherever the sea takes someone (a swamped boat, a diver who doesn't surface, a child off a reef), he goes into the water. The Low Reaches have an anonymous fisher's tale about **a big man in a straw cape** who pulls the drowning out and is gone before anyone can thank him. Nobody connects the tale with the dead captain, and nobody hails him with it (0017's Ch5 watch holds).
- **He did not watch over Asha or visit Rhen.** When a new Cantor was named, he started walking toward Corrow, because the storm always breaks at Coilspire during a Rising, and he meant to be at the stair. At Pemakar (Ch5) he met the pilgrimage on its way there. He saw Imra's face, then heard Rhen's surname, and walked with them because they were going where he was going.
- **Want:** to be at the stair when the sea comes. **Need:** to accept that no wall and no stair will ever get everyone up, and to come off the wall. The Unbinding is the first thing in a thousand years that does get everyone up, so his watch ends there.
- Bible §5 widens the unrisen rule from "an unfinished promise" to "something unfinished: a promise, a duty, a watch". Every unrisen is still something not yet finished.

### (c) Face and costume cues
- **No mask and no covered lower face. Both eyes are clear.** His face is bare and sea-weathered: broad, with deep squint-lines, a nose broken flat years before he died, clear grey eyes, grey-black hair cropped like a soldier's, and a short beard.
- **His scars are on his hands and forearms**, not his face. They are the rope-burns from the stair's hand-line, spiralling from palm to elbow, and they are his death-wounds. They never healed, they leak light in dim scenes (the gold shader cue, unchanged), and they are why his motes leave **his hands** in Ch15.
- **New asymmetry:** his **left forearm is wrapped** from knuckle to elbow in tarred hauling-cloth, because that hand took the rope's full run. The right forearm is bare, showing the paler spiral scar. The Stillguard captain's knotted cord on the right hip stays.
- Unchanged: the palm-fibre rain-cape, the anchor-cleaver, and the IP note (no hat, no coat worn off one arm, no eyewear, no gourd).

### (d) Knock-ons, chapter by chapter
- **Ch5:** his one line to Asha is **"I held a stair for your mother once."** (It replaces "I travelled with your mother.") He says nothing more, and nothing to Rhen.
- **Ch6:** he keeps his back to Sefir's barge because the Order knows his face (he is the dead Hero of Corrow) and he knows what the Stilling is. Sefir's eyes rest on him for half a beat (unchanged).
- **Ch8:** during the evacuation, Kerrid works the **foot** of the Fisher Stair, waist-deep, pushing the last climbers up. An old Corrow fishwife on the stair stares at him and says "Captain?" He says nothing and goes down a step. He comes up the stair last, and that is why he is there to pull Rhen off the wall's edge by the collar. He is gone from Corrow before the Ebbing (the "never near the Ebbing" clue). This is the place he died, but the player doesn't know it yet, and nothing stages it as a return.
- **Ch12:** Toma notices the breath. Toma knows the captain's story because every Stillguard cadet learns the Fisher Stair of 1001.
- **Ch15 (the confession):** he tells them what happened **at Corrow**: the week as Imra and Dace's host, the gate held open, the line through his hands, Dace's reach, and the watch he never came off. To Asha he says the hard part: "I got your mother up the stair. That's all I did. I got her up so she could walk to the Choir." To Rhen: "Your father reached back for me. He missed. I don't think he forgave himself." There are no promises and nothing about the Choir, because he wasn't there.
- **Ch16:** he does **not** find the spot where he died, because it is at Corrow. At the Rim he walks **last** in the line, as on a stair, and wades for days toward the place he once sent Imra. At the Choir, Kesh's memory shows what the Hush does, and Kerrid learns what he saved Imra for. His confrontation with Sefir is still fury with no shouting, but its content is new: "I carried her up so you could do this." Then he holds the line while the party escapes, first down and last up.
- **Act III:** he leads them into the storm, first down the stair.
- **Sefir:** no longer his killer. To Sefir he is the Order's dead Hero of Corrow, who kept walking, and Sefir finds him interesting rather than frightening.

### (e) The Ending order: Imra, then Dace, then Kerrid last
- **E1 Imra → R2 → E2 Dace → R3 → E3 Kerrid → R4.** Kerrid goes **last** of the three, by the stair-law: the captain comes off the wall last. Everyone was released at H09 (act3_ending §0 rule 7). An unrisen comes apart slowly, from his wounds outward, and his scene is the end of that. Nothing is released after H09. His first line, the Stillguard all-clear **"Stair's clear."**, is a report that nobody is left below. It is not a release.
- His last line is unchanged, and it is stronger after Dace's scene, because the player has just seen Dace: "Stand up straight. Your father slouched." He fixes Rhen's collar the way he grabbed it at Corrow, and his shadow cue (N-16) is kept.
- Imra must still come before Dace, because Dace's hands are only free once she has gone (the old E3 staging, now E2).
- Rhen's arc is kept. The father-shaped man stays until the father has gone, and then goes too. Kerrid's scene is no longer a rehearsal for losing Dace. It is the first goodbye Rhen says after learning how, and it is said with a joke between them, not a breakdown.

### (f) Liss: not the chief's daughter, and Tobin is not an heir
- **Hollowmere has no chief.** It is governed by its council of hull-captains. Liss's father is the flotilla's **dock-master**, keeper of the dry-dock berths, which is why Dace's unfinished hull is in his keeping.
- **Tobin was not an heir. He volunteered.** When the Order asked the council for a Saltwright groom for a Warden's daughter, he offered himself so that the council wouldn't have to choose anyone. The peace is still his people's, and his death still costs them it.
- **Accepted residual: the ship comes from her people's haven.** The Kite is mandated (SPEC §3.5). Ours is Dace's hull, kept in a berth by a dock-master who isn't a chief, and finished by the hero's father's notes after the Choir, so it is not handed over by the heroine's people at their home.

### (g) The Prologue line is replaced (human-approved, relayed by the Lead)
The old line, "Listen. This might be the last time I tell it.", is close in sense to the inspiring game's opening line. Three candidates in Rhen's voice that keep its intent (a frame narrator, in medias res, the sense that this might be an ending):
1. *"Oil's low. I'll tell it while the wick lasts."* It ties to the oil lamp (N-02) and Mother Kell's wick, but it is a time limit rather than a loss, and the wick has only just caught in the shot.
2. *"Let me get this lit first. Then I'll start with the part where I was still laughing."* It is in his voice and sets up the arc, but it is 18 words of setup and the joke softens the vigil.
3. *"Everyone I'm going to tell you about is still here tonight. Remember that, later."* **(Chosen.)**

**Why 3:** it is the only one that names what the frame is really about. All seven are on the plaza, apart, at the end of the road. "Still here" quietly promises that some of them won't be, and "Remember that, later" asks the player to hold the frame, which is the game's own question about holding versus letting go. At Ch19 the same line plays again and now hurts: Kerrid, Imra and Dace are about to go. It doesn't use "listen", "story" or "chance", and nothing in it repeats the inspiring game's opening. It is 14 words, inside the voice guide's limit.

The line appears only in story docs. There is no `.dlg` or loc key for it yet. The SPEC §3.5 amendment is the Lead's to apply.

### (h) Ch7: the newcomer for an island side is accepted, and Oruvel's side does not win the cup
- **Accepted residual (Lead):** the newcomer plays for a small island side that is a player short. The tournament is mandated (SPEC §3.5, §6.10). The side is Oruvel's, a town the player watched drown. Rhen isn't a newcomer to Reefball or its star; he is filling a dead boy's place. Nobody on the side is a guardian or a captain from the party.
- **Oruvel's side does not win the cup.** The story match is their opening round. Win or lose (the result gives a reward only and never branches the story), **Tavi withdraws the side after it** to go home and search what is left of Oruvel. The cup goes on without them and is won offscreen by Tamsule's own side, mentioned once in passing. That is "for the ones who didn't come up": they played once, for their dead, and went home. No long-losing team wins its first cup.

### (i) Ch6: "I knelt beside your mother" is accepted, with a reason (Lead)
The cleric's warm first meeting invokes the heroine's spent parent, as in the inspiring game's introduction. We keep it because it lands differently in three ways:
- it is spoken at a burning, unasked, as a mercy that costs him nothing, with no crowd and no ceremony;
- it is a true statement that Ch16 turns: he knelt beside Imra at the Choir while she was made the heart;
- Kerrid, who got her up the stair so she could get there, has his back to the barge while Sefir says it.
