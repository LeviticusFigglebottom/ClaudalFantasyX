## Tuning log

### 2026-10-22: M1 sandbox softened (M1-CMB-05, combat-stream-2)

The sandbox was harsh: a naive party never won and wiped in about two rounds. The target is naive 5–15% with the role-answer swap at 85% or more. Enemy damage cuts alone barely moved naive, because naive spends the fight on the Reef Crab (the first foe, and the bulkiest) while the Gull Harrier's rank-2 Dive Rake (AGI 24) does most of the damage. So the fix trims the crab's bulk and the harrier's bite, with a light cut to the other two attackers.

What the numbers say:
- Naive is now in band, and a naive win still takes about 17 actions with roughly two members down.

The swap reward is now the 14-point gap from lowest HP to role answer (it was 28). ### 2026-10-23: Swell in the simulator (M2-CMB-01, worktree-agent-a670939c5cb593976)

### 2026-10-23: the masher's Swell and Shallows Verse (M2-NIT-03, agent-a14a20707d2ef004e)

Review sweep M found that a masher who presses the Swell row wins 37.5% against the 5–15% naive band, mostly through Shallows Verse (power 12, every ally, no MP: 391 HP per ally at good, nearly three free Chorus Mends). Three changes follow.

- **A report-only `naive_swell` policy.** This is `naive` plus the Swell row, with a damage technique aimed at the first foe, where the cursor starts. It now appears in every suite next to `naive`, with decision lines at 35% (starting gear) and 50% (tier-1) in balance_targets.md.
- **Shallows Verse: power 12 → 4.** Everything else on the row is unchanged.
- **Policy fixes.** In `role_answer` and `smart`, the Swell now takes the answered foe (`swell_focus`) and waits behind the free swap to the answer.

| naive_swell | 37.9 / 49.5 | **32.1 / 47.7** | 26.4 / 43.5 | 34.7 / 48.7 |
| lowest_hp | 88.5 / 91.9 | **87.7 / 91.8** | 86.5 / 91.2 | 87.9 / 91.9 |
| role_answer | 94.2 / 98.1 | **97.2 / 98.5** | 97.2 / 98.5 | 97.2 / 98.5 |

Every band holds at every quality, and both ease ceilings hold (`sim.py --check`).

**Why power, and why 4.** I swept the Verse's power, rank, target and riders, and Ballast's rate (`naive_swell`, starting gear; 1000 seeds, except powers 1 to 3 at 400):

| Verse power (good → healed per ally) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | **4 (115)** | 5 (161) | 6 (184) | 12 (391) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive_swell win % | 19.8 | 20.5 | 24.8 | 29.0 | **32.1** | 34.7 | 35.7 | 37.9 |

- **The Verse's value to a masher is a second life, not its size.** Ballast fills only after about 83% of max HP is lost. Naive never heals, so when Asha's gauge fills she is nearly down, and any party heal brings her back. That is why the curve is steep up to about power 4 and nearly flat after it. A single-ally Verse scores the same as the every-ally one (the hurt ally it picks is Asha). Rank 3 to 6 moves nothing, and a Regen rider adds about 3 points.
- **Ballast's rate is a cliff, not a dial.** At 100 or less no gauge fills before a KO, so nobody's Swell fires. At 105, 110 and 115 the masher wins 17, 23 and 32%, but the careful policies lose their Swells too: lowest HP unleashes 0.40, 0.20 and 0.07 times a fight instead of 0.52. That cost hits all four heroes, not just the Verse. It also works only because the simulator starts every gauge empty. In play, gauges carry between fights (M2-ARC-01), so a slower fill only moves the Verse to the next fight. Rejected.
- **Power 4 is where the input still decides.** Against Chorus Mend (138 per ally from Asha, 14 MP, rank 4), the Verse heals 92 when missed and 115 at good, so it is below a Chorus Mend there. Only a perfect chant beats it, at 161. That is the review's "heal the party less than a full Chorus Mend" for anyone who does not nail the chant. The excitement is now in the chant: perfect is 75% more than missed, and it is the only free heal that beats the paid one. Power 3 would get the masher under 30% (29.0), but then no grade would reach a Chorus Mend and the input would stop mattering.
- **Honest residual: 32.1% is above the review's "about 30%".** I accept it deliberately and set the decision line at 35%. Lamp Dive alone already takes a masher to 19.8%, which is the comeback Swell is for. The Verse's extra lift fell from about 18 points to about 12, close to Lamp Dive's 9. So the Verse is no longer the one Swell a masher cannot lose with. The rest is the second-life loop. Lowering the numbers cannot remove it, only a structural change can: a Verse that heals everyone but the singer (a new target kind), or Asha filling from her allies' hurt (a per-hero default mode, Tether-like). Both are design follow-ups (task notes), not part of this nit.
- **Role answer was not hurt by Swell.** The 3 points it lost at miss and good (M2-CMB-01 entry above) were the technique pre-empting the free swap to Toma or Ysolde. Aiming at the answered foe alone changed nothing, because Rhen answers the Harrier, which is usually the weakest foe anyway. With the swap first, role answer at good equals its Swell-off number (97.2 vs 97.1).
- **Tether's unlock count: 12 → 40.** With Rhen's Swell alone, a careful player took about 2 counted hits per sandbox fight, so 12 meant about six fights. With all four heroes counting it is 6.1 to 7.1 hits (300 seeds), so Tether came in the second fight. 40 restores about six (formulas.md § Swell).

- **It removes the loop rather than moving it.** Under (a), the song arrives when the singer is nearly down and saves only the others. - **It fits the story** (pillars 1 and 6). The Cantor's song is for the others and never for herself, which is the bargain the Hush makes of every Cantor. The target pick shows exactly who it reaches ("All other allies"; pillar 4).

### 2026-10-23: the Drownbell (M2-FP-06, first-playable stream)

The first boss suite with data. Encounter `drownbell` (the suite's encounter was renamed from the placeholder `boss_drownbell` to the id Cutscene B starts), party Rhen, Asha and Toma with no reserve, loadout `ch03_drownbell` (the tier-1 gear and consumables plus the flag `ch03_ilun_bound`, so Asha knows Call Ilun). Mechanics: battle_ctb.md § The Drownbell. Bands: naive 0–10%, role answer 70–90%, smart 80–95%, p90 ≤ 60 actions.

**Final numbers** (seeds 1..N, `python3 tools/balance/sim.py`):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| naive | 0.0% | 0.0% | 26.7 / 32 | 3.00 | 0 |
| role answer | 79.7% | 80.3% | 31.8 / 35 | 0.87 | 239 |
| smart | 89.6% | 90.4% | 39.3 / 51 | 0.36 | 269 |

**How it was tuned.** An in-memory sweep (bell HP, phase threshold, Hood Slam, Toll and Floodtide power) at 100–300 seeds per point, then the official run. What the sweep showed:
- **Role answer and smart are coupled.** `role_answer` never heals, so it only wins when the bell's total damage stays under the party's HP pool (about 1360). Every point of steady damage that `smart` shrugs off with Mend costs `role_answer` a lot. Both bands only hold with a light steady threat (Hood Slam power 4, about 90 damage) and the danger concentrated where the answer matters.
- **The danger is the Flood.** *Floodtide* (power 13 on all three, every bell turn while the field is up) is what sorts the policies. Naive never calls Ilun and drowns (0% in every configuration tried). `role_answer` calls Ilun on Asha's first phase-2 turn. `smart` heals first when someone is below 45%, and that delay under Floodtide is most of its 10% losses: the player lesson the tutorial line states ("The water is feeding it. Call on Ilun.").
- **The rim works as designed.** With the gather on the bell's first turn, Rhen's fast strikes (Quick Hit 116 ticks, Attack 174) break the rim before the toll in every `smart` and `role_answer` fight (1000 of 1000 breaks, no toll landed). Naive Rhen breaks it less often, because Asha's and Toma's Attacks (240 and 300 ticks) never count and naive does not spend Quick Hits: the toll lands in about half of naive fights (790 tolls in 1000).
- Phase threshold 40% (not 50%): a longer phase 1 so the gather/toll cycle shows at least once, and a shorter Flood.
- Bell 3000 HP, agility 12, DEF 30, delay-immune. Polyps 180 HP, *Feeding Tide* power 20 (about 290 per heal, more than the party deals per bell turn).

**Not modelled by the simulator:** Toma's Lattice skills (Shield Wall halves the physical Toll; Challenge pulls the Hood Slams), Swell techniques, and the player reading the timeline to time Quick Hits. The bands are for the bare-Lattice party, like the sandbox suites; a full-Lattice run is still the open follow-up from M1-CMB-05.

**A sim fix on the way:** `BattleSim.run_one` passed an unset `party_modifier` into `EquipmentEffects.compose`, which (since M1-PRG-03) logs an error for every party member in every geared run. It now composes only valid hooks. No numbers moved.
