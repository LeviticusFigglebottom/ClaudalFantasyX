# M1-RND-15 criterion 4: prewarm worst frame and Low-TAA foliage ghosting, on the GPU (2026-10-23)

Machine: AMD RX 9070 XT, Godot 4.6.3-stable Forward+/Vulkan, Windows 10. Branch `gpu/rnd-15-prewarm`.
Load: CPU ~5 %, memory ~60 %, and the GPU otherwise idle. One measurement at a time.

## (a) The worst frame during prewarm, against SPEC §4.2's 50 ms

**Tool.** `tools/gpu_bench/prewarm_bench.tscn`, with two options added here:
- **Per-frame wall-time trace** of the prewarm, the spawn frame included (`prewarm_frames`, `prewarm_wall_max_ms`,
  `prewarm_trace_ms` in the CSV). The prewarm's own `max_frame_ms` (130–150 ms on every run) misses its first frame
  and reads a clamped delta.
- **`--pre-scene`.** A field scene (Haluna) plays for 120 frames first, because a battle transition always follows one.
  Without it the first prewarm frame also pays the engine's first-ever TAA frame in the process (~0.9 s, warm cache),
  which a running game paid long before.

"Cold" means `user://shader_cache` and `user://vulkan` were cleared first; "warm" means a second run.

### Before (as merged: batch 12, all materials loaded in `start()`)

Without a pre-scene, the worst prewarm frame was 1.37 s (`asha_stage`) and 2.1 s (`haluna_mood`), cold or warm, and
**at any `materials_per_frame` (1, 2, 4 or 12)**. The later frames were ~1 ms. So the batch size wasn't what cost
time; the frame that created the stage was.

That frame broke down as follows:
- **The game viewport's first TAA frame, ~0.9 s.** It moves to the scene's first frame if the stage has no TAA, so
  it's a one-time cost that `--pre-scene` removes.
- **`start()` loading every registered material and shader at once, 43–324 ms of CPU.** One material load compiles
  its shader, up to ~35 ms each.
- **The stage's own set-up.** The stage is already 64 × 64; 256 and 640 px measured the same, so size isn't a lever.

Staging FSR2 as TAA on the stage was tried: there was no change after a pre-scene, so it was reverted.

### Change

- **`ShaderPrewarm.start()` now only lists the registry entries.** Each entry is loaded and built in its batch
  (`_build_entry`), so material loads are time-sliced too.
- **The first tick never halves the batch** (review-sweep-J S3). That frame carries `start()` and the stage set-up.
- **`materials_per_frame` 12 → 2** (data). `slice_budget_ms` stays 33.

### After (`--pre-scene` = Haluna, then the battle-side scene; worst prewarm frame, ms)

| Incoming scene | Batch | Cold | Warm | Frames staged |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| asha_stage (the hero; battle stand-in) | 12 | 314 | 227 | 6 |
| asha_stage | 4 | 323 | 230 | 6 |
| asha_stage | **2** | 312 | 229 | 13 |
| undertow_test (heaviest shaders) | 12 | 1578 | 1303 | 6 |
| undertow_test | 4 | 965 | 810 | 16 |
| undertow_test | **2** | **618** | **491** | 21 |

The SceneRouter waits at most 120 frames; 21 is well inside that. The other prewarm frames are mostly under 10 ms.
Undertow at batch 2 still has a run of 90–280 ms frames, one per heavy body or sea material.

### Verdict

**Over 50 ms on every scene and preset, and the slice constants cannot fix it.** The floor has two parts:
1. **Stage set-up and first render, ~230 ms (warm) to ~310 ms (cold)**, which comes before any material is drawn.
2. **One material's load, up to ~90–430 ms**, mostly in the Undertow body and sea variants. That's a single shader
   compile on load, so it can't be split further.

Low, High and Ultra behave the same; the preset doesn't change these costs.

Recommendations (not done here; they need the Lead or Systems):
- **Ship precompiled shaders.** Godot 4.5+'s shader baker runs at export (Project → Export → "Shader Baker"). It
  removes the on-load compile of part 2 in release builds. These measurements are the editor or uncompiled path, so
  they are the worst case.
- **Keep one prewarm stage alive from boot** (or warm at the title or loading screen). Part 1 is then paid once, under
  a loading screen, not at each battle transition.
- **Register only the active tier's variants** (the registry notes already ask for this). Undertow alone stages 13
  materials across tiers; one tier would be about a quarter of that.

## (b) Low-preset TAA foliage ghosting, wind on, 30 fps

**Tool.** `tools/gpu_bench/aa_shimmer.tscn`, with `--wind`, `--scene` and `--camera` added. Runs use
`--fixed-fps 30` and no camera pan, so each frame advances the wind clock by exactly 1/30 s. Each pair is TAA against
no AA at the same wind instants. `aa_shimmer.py --ghost` gives the mean and p99 linear difference over the
reference's busy (edge) pixels. A still pair gives TAA's static softening, so the excess with the wind on is the
ghost. Preset Low (FSR1 + TAA), 1080p, 30 frames after a 60-frame warm-up.

| Scene / camera | Wind | Motion (no-AA flicker, busy px) | TAA vs no AA, mean | p99 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| vegetation Wide | still | 0.000 | 0.115 | 0.445 |
| vegetation Wide | **on** | 0.076 | **0.149** | 0.534 |
| vegetation FrangipaniClose | still | – | 0.113 | 0.421 |
| vegetation FrangipaniClose | **on** | 0.082 | **0.158** | 0.579 |
| haluna_mood H01 | on (always) | 0.016 | 0.095 | 0.460 |
| haluna_mood H02 | on (always) | 0.013 | 0.129 | 0.566 |

**Reading.**
- With the wind on, the TAA image departs from the instant 30–40 % more than its static softening does. That's
  +0.034 for Wide and +0.045 for FrangipaniClose.
- In a crop (FrangipaniClose, frame 20) the ghost shows as softened blades and faint double edges where blades cross
  quickly. **There are no long trails or smears**, and the flowers and trunks hold.
- Haluna's foliage moves less in frame (palms at range), and its pairs sit at the vegetation scene's still level or
  just above.
- **Verdict: acceptable for Low.** The ghost is mild and reads as motion blur at 30 fps. Low stays on TAA: it removed
  65 % of the alpha-test shimmer (M1-RND-07), which matters more. Nothing to tune.
