# M1-RND-27: prewarm follow-ups (ADR 0029's hidden-frame limit, the Undertow compute pipeline) (2026-10-24)

Machine: RX 9070 XT, Godot 4.6.3. Branch `gpu/adr-0029-prewarm-followups`, cut from the integration branch at 9db3f08.
ADR 0029 (SPEC v1.3 §4.2) sets the limit: no hidden frame over 1 s. Tool: `tools/gpu_bench/prewarm_bench.tscn`.
`--resident` runs the boot warm-up as boot does, and the new `--trace` prints every boot frame and what it drew.
"Cold" means this project's `shader_cache` and `vulkan` caches were cleared before the run.

## 1. The boot warm-up: every frame under 1 s

**What the worst frame was.** The trace showed that it wasn't a shader at all. It was the stage's first frame:
the process's first 3D render. With nothing drawn (an experiment), that frame alone took 958 ms cold. TAA, the
upscaler and the renderer's base pipelines all compiled in it. The first batch (the Undertow body and spray) came
on top of it.

**Three changes:**
- **Bring-up.** A boot run draws its stage plain first, then turns the game viewport's TAA, scaling mode, MSAA and
  screen-space AA on one a frame, before staging anything. Only the features the game viewport uses are steps.
- **Residents load in their batch.** `spawn_resident` no longer loads every resident shader in the spawn frame;
  each is held as its batch loads it. The spawn frame went from 94–120 ms to 3 ms.
- **The Undertow body's compute twin** (criterion 2 below). On High and Medium the spatial body shader never
  draws, so the boot no longer compiles it: about 0.2 s of wasted work in one frame.

**Boot warm-up, worst frame** (ms; `vegetation_test` as the bench's scene; each column is a separate process):

| Preset | Before, cold | After, cold (2 runs) | After, warm (2 runs) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 1118 | 862, 865 | 470, 471 |
| Medium | 1055 | – | – |
| High | 1182–1197 (3 runs) | 885, 875; final code 872, 874 | 555, 540; final 549, 544 |
| Ultra | 1055 | 863, 888 | 657, 645 |

- **Before**, warm High was 741–770 ms. These runs are mine, on the same bench and method. RND-22 recorded
  0.53–1.66 s over its 18 runs.
- **After**, every run is under 1 s: 12 of 12 across presets, plus the final-code check. The warm-up as a whole
  takes 1.5–2.0 s warm and 2.5–2.8 s cold.
- **The remaining worst frame is the plain first 3D frame.** That's the renderer's own first-use pipelines, about
  0.86 s cold and 0.47–0.66 s warm. It can't be split any further from the project side.
  - **Margin:** 11 % under the limit on this CPU. A CPU about 15 % slower at shader compiles would cross it.
  - **The real fix is follow-up 1** (the export shader baker), which pre-builds exactly these.

## 2. The Undertow compute pipeline is on the prewarm list (criterion 2)

- **Registry.** The three spatial body entries now carry `"compute_twin": true`. When the preset's storm tier
  marches the body in compute, the stage creates the compute pipeline instead of drawing the spatial shader. That's
  `ShaderPrewarm.undertow_uses_compute`: a RenderingDevice exists, and `body_scale` is under 1 (Medium and High).
- **The warm cache.** The twin is created by `UndertowBodyEffect.warm_pipeline`, on the render thread, and held for
  the session. The effect's `_ensure_pipeline` takes it from that cache and never frees it.
- **Ultra** (full-resolution body) and devices without RD still draw the spatial body.
- **Tests:** the twins exist on disk, and without a device the spatial body is staged as before.
- **The storm scene's transition** (High, boot first):
  - `start()` went from 177 ms cold (and 144 warm) to 6–9 ms cold.
  - The worst prewarm frame went from 144 to 127 ms cold, and from 143 to 79 ms warm.

## 3. Haluna's harvest from its SceneState (follow-up 2)

**The harvest** now reads the scene's `SceneState`, without instancing it. It also reads the states of the scenes
the zone instances, with the instancing scene's overrides on their root.

**Instancing wasn't the cost.** The trace found the real one: three primitive meshes on Haluna's save shrine (a
CylinderMesh, a SphereMesh and a TorusMesh).
- A PrimitiveMesh builds its arrays on its first surface query, or when it's assigned to a node.
- Building them builds its StandardMaterial3D's shader: about 75 ms each, synchronously in `start()`.

**The fix:**
- The harvest reads a PrimitiveMesh's `material` directly.
- It queues each draw as `[material, mesh, multimesh]`, and the draw is built in its batch, like a registry entry.

**Haluna zone entry** (High, boot first; ms):

| | `start()` cold | `start()` warm | Worst prewarm frame, cold / warm |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Before | 664 | 506 | 146 / 150 |
| After | **353** | **270** | 141 / 143 |

**What's left in `start()`** (270–350 ms) is its first batch: two registry materials whose loads compile their
shaders. The router's swap frame is hidden, and it's well under 1 s. The time-slicing tests expect the first batch
in `start()`, so it stays there.

Measured cold on `undertow_test` at High, boot first:

|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 (kept) | 127 ms | 15 |

## 5. The export shader baker (follow-up 1): not done

There are no export templates on this PC; `%APPDATA%/Godot/export_templates` is empty. The baker needs a release
export, which needs Godot 4.6.3's export templates.
- **What it would fix:** the boot's plain first frame (0.86 s cold) and most of each remaining compile.
- **What it needs:** the templates installed (a large download from godotengine.org, Godot's official source),
  then a run of `godot --export-release` with `shader_baker/enabled` in export_presets.cfg.
- **I didn't download them** without the Lead's or the human's say-so. It's a one-line approval.
