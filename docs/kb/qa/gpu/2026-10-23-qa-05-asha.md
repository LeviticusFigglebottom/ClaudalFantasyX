# M1-QA-05: Asha (MPFB2 rebuild) on a real GPU: renderer artefact or asset? (2026-10-23)

**Setup.** AMD RX 9070 XT, official Godot 4.6.3-stable, Vulkan; see [`2026-10-23-gpu-report.md`](2026-10-23-gpu-report.md). Branch `gpu/qa-05-asha-gpu` from `origin/claude/new-session-mj8c3c`, which has M1-CHR-03.

**Captures.** Every `asha_*` and `char_*` shot (14) was rendered three ways. Baselines were **not** modified.
- **Default:** the capture rig with the scene's own settings, as the lavapipe baselines were made (`tools/gpu_bench/capture_gpu.sh`).
- **High** and **Ultra:** the presets applied through GraphicsSettings, plus the matching character shader tier (`tools/gpu_bench/capture_preset.tscn`, 60 settle frames).
- **2× resolution, High:** the six face and body shots, for close inspection.
- **Diagnosis renders:** `capture_preset --light-set=…` / `--mat-set=…` change one thing at a time.

## Verdict in one line

**None of the five defects is a renderer artefact.** At scene settings the GPU matches lavapipe within RMSE 0.0011–0.0057 on every shot. The cheek banding and the blotchy face shadows are a **shadow-settings issue**, the shadow-terminator problem on a low-poly face, which normal bias fixes. The lip seam, the plastic costume and the blocky hair are **asset / look-dev faults**.

## Defect classification

| Defect | Class | Evidence (2× High, `asha_face_neutral`, one change at a time) | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Diagonal banding on the shadow-side cheek** | **Shadow settings**, triggered by geometry (the shadow-terminator artefact) | Sun shadows off: gone. `shadow_normal_bias` 1.0 → 3.0: gone (the nose's real cast shadow stays). Finer texels (`directional_shadow_max_distance` 8 → 2 m): **worse**, a sharper sawtooth that follows the triangles. Godot's normal bias scales with texel size, so finer shadows shrink it. Skin detail normal off: unchanged. Curvature/scatter off: unchanged. Identical on lavapipe. | **Now (Rendering/Cinematics rigs):** `shadow_normal_bias` 2.5–3.0 on character key lights. At body scale, 3.0 shows no peter-panning: feet and staff contact shadows hold (`asha_q34`). **Asset (CHR-04):** more face density on the cheeks and jaw (or a subdivided head for dialogue), so smooth normals and flat triangles disagree less. |
| **Harsh, blotchy self-shadowing on the face** | **Shadow settings** (same cause) | The same experiments: the blotches are shadow-acne islands where adjacent triangles alternate in and out of the shadow. Normal bias 3.0 removes them. | As above. Soft-shadow quality doesn't fix it (High and Ultra both show it). |
| **Dark lip seam** | **Asset** | Shadows off: the seam turns into a *light* sliver. There's a small gap between the lips showing the mouth interior, which shadow then (correctly) darkens. Also, in every render: a dark horizontal crease line across the lower lip and strong vertical lip striations, from the lip albedo/normal. | **CHR-04:** close the lip contact in the neutral pose (or seal the gap with the mouth-interior/occlusion geometry), soften the lower-lip crease in the albedo, and reduce the lip-line normal strength. |
| **Glossy, plastic-looking costume** | **Asset / look-dev** (per spec, overdone) | asha.md specifies the vest as "black lacquered leather… B lacquer (whole vest)". Measured in `asha_cloth_clothmask.png`: **100 %** of the dark vest texels have lacquer at full strength, with sheen 0. The cloth shader's lacquer is a clear coat at roughness 0.06, so the whole vest is a flawless 0.06 coat with nothing breaking it up. That's what reads as plastic. Same on every preset and on lavapipe. | **CHR-04 / Art:** lacquer mask 0.6–0.8 with wear (edges, folds, the toggles), a leather-grain normal under the coat, sheen 0.1–0.2 on the leather. **Rendering:** expose the lacquer coat roughness, now a shader constant of 0.06, as a material uniform so the vest can sit around 0.12–0.18 without touching the shader. |
| **Blocky hair cards** | **Asset** | Rectangular blocks of straight strokes with hard card borders. Identical at High and Ultra (TAA/FSR2 versus TAA native), so AA, shadows and resolution don't change it. | **CHR-04:** more and narrower cards per clump, tapered strand alpha (root-to-tip and edge falloff in `*_hair` A), varied `*_hairflow`, and a scalp hair albedo under the cards so gaps read as hair, not skin. The hair contract in character_shading.md already describes the channels. |

## Recommendations

**Character (M1-CHR-04):**
1. Face topology: more density across the cheeks and jaw (the terminator).
2. Lips: close the contact; soften the crease and striations.
3. Vest lacquer: mask below 1 with wear, a leather grain normal, a little sheen.
4. Hair cards: density, taper, flow variation, scalp texture.

**Rendering:**
1. **Character lighting rule** (now in character_shading.md, "Shadows on characters"): `shadow_normal_bias` 2.5–3.0 on lights that key a face. Don't fix banding by shrinking the shadow max distance; with this mesh that makes it worse.
2. Expose `lacquer_coat_roughness` (and maybe `lacquer_base_roughness`) as cloth-shader uniforms.
3. Optionally, dialogue rigs (Cinematics) put the key light's normal bias in their data. The graphics presets don't own per-light bias.

## Correction (M1-CHR-04, 2026-10-23)

The table and Rendering recommendation 2 above say the lacquer coat roughness is a shader constant. It is not:
`character_cloth_body.gdshaderinc` already declares `lacquer_base_roughness`, `lacquer_coat_roughness` and
`lacquer_coat_strength` as uniforms. Asha's `cloth.tres` just never set them, so it ran on the hard-lacquer defaults.
M1-CHR-04 sets them in the material (base 0.55, coat 0.16, strength 0.55); no shader change was needed.
