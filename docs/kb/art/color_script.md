# Color Script

Owner: Art Director (M1-ART-01). Readers: Rendering (lighting, LUTs, glimmer, water), Environment (zone lighting and shots), Cinematics (story-locked time of day). SPEC §7.2, §8.1.

This file defines one key frame per chapter. Each frame is the **target that composed shots are reviewed against** (SPEC §8.4). Until paintovers or shader-only mood frames exist, the numbers below *are* the color script. When Rendering builds a mood frame, it saves the frame's environment as `game/levels/_mood/<frame_id>.tres` and the review screenshot as a baseline. Every value here can be tuned in review, but tune the frame and record the change, not the individual shot.

## Conventions (read before using any number)

- **Hex is sRGB-encoded**: it's what you type into Godot's color picker. **`lin (r, g, b)`** is the sRGB transfer function applied to that hex, which is what the shader receives. Kelvin values are correlated color temperature. "Off-locus" means the color is too green or too magenta to have a meaningful blackbody temperature, which is deliberate for teal fills and glimmer.
- **Every hex in this file is an input** (light color, sky color, fog albedo, shader color), not what the screen shows. What the screen shows is the palette's `onscreen_hex`, measured on the swatch card at the frame's key product `k` (see [README.md](README.md), "Two kinds of colour value"). Screenshots are reviewed against the on-screen values.
- **Key** is the `DirectionalLight3D` (sun or moon) or, when the sun is below the horizon or the scene is enclosed, the dominant practical source named in "Time of day". Its color is derived from the stated blackbody temperature so the two always agree.
- **Energies** are Godot light energy with physical light units **off** (project default, kept by [ADR 0014](../decisions/0014-light-units-exposure.md)). Fill means sky and ambient contribution (`Environment.ambient_light_energy` or the sky contribution), and rim means a cinematic `DirectionalLight3D` or `SpotLight3D` placed for the shot and culled to characters (light layer 2).
- **Sun angles:** elevation in degrees above the horizon. Azimuth in degrees clockwise from world north, where **north = −Z** in every level scene. So 90 = east (+X), 180 = south (+Z), 270 = west (−X).
- **Fog density** is `Environment.volumetric_fog_density`. Use the albedo given and an emission of 0 unless the frame says otherwise.
- **Water** names a **water look**, validated in the water test scene (see "Water looks" below), and gives its `absorption_scale`. That multiplies the shared base `absorption_per_m` = (2.0, 0.5, 0.42) m⁻¹. These are look values tuned against the tonemap, not physical seawater: the v0 physical triples (R 0.45, G 0.065, B 0.045) rendered near-white (M1-ART-01 review). σ is the product the shader receives (Beer–Lambert, `transmittance = exp(-σ · path)`). The deep color is `col_deep`, the scattered color at depth > 15 m.
- **Glimmer** names a swatch from [`palette.json`](palette.json). Emission energy is the mote material's `emission_energy_multiplier` at the mote core, before bloom.
- **Exposure** is `Environment.tonemap_exposure`, the only exposure control ([ADR 0014](../decisions/0014-light-units-exposure.md)). The EV100 in parentheses is the v0 intent, kept for traceability and never typed in. It was converted as `0.65 × 2^((15 − EV100) / 10)`, rounded to 0.05, which passes through Rendering's validated 0.65 (noon) and 1.1 (night). **Key product** `k` = exposure × key energy: it tells you which palette `onscreen_hex` column (−1 / 0 / +1 = k 0.5 / 1 / 2) a front-lit surface reads in. For example, `ch02_noon` is 0.65 × 3.2 = 2.1, so +1 (fill adds a little more). **S-curve** is LUT contrast strength from 0 (flat) to 1 (strong). **Saturation** is a multiplier applied in the LUT after tonemapping.
- **Tonemapper:** AgX for every frame (filmic roll-off, no hue skew in saturated highlights), `tonemap_agx_white` 12, `tonemap_agx_contrast` 1.25 (the engine default; don't change it per frame). The LUT does the grading after it. Measured mapping at exposure 1: linear 0.18 → `#757575` (about the same as plain sRGB encoding at mid grey), 1.0 → `#CBCBCB` (80%), 2.0 → `#E1E1E1`, 4.4 → `#F2F2F2`, ≥ 12 → `#FFFFFF`. Saturated colors are pulled toward white as they brighten: `#3FD0C9` at k 1 reads `#6FB8B5`.
- **LUT ids** map to `game/shaders/luts/<lut_id>.png` (32³ strip), generated by Rendering's Python LUT tool from the intent text. LUTs are never hand-painted.
- **Visible-sky lift is a per-zone exception (Haluna `ch02_noon`, M1-RND-12).** Haluna's sky material sets `background_energy` 1.5: the sky the camera sees sits **1.5× above the sky its water reflects and its fill comes from** (the radiance cubemap keeps `sky_energy` 1.0, so `lagoon_clear` and every ground value are unchanged). Don't copy the value into another zone without re-validating its water look: the horizon sky and the grazing-angle sea would step apart. For the signature sky, prefer moving the lift into `sky_energy` and re-validating the water (review-sweep-H R5).

## The arc in one line (SPEC §7.2)

**Act I is warm, high-noon festival. Act II turns overcast, stormy, moonlit, and cold blue stone. Act III moves through dusk gold, then the glimmer abyss, then total darkness under stars.**

Measured as numbers across the frames below, the key light runs ~5,600 K at noon in Act I, ~6,500–12,000 K or moonlight in Act II, ~2,500–3,400 K dusk in Act III, and starlight in the final frame. Saturation peaks at ×1.18 (Ch7, Tidecup) and bottoms out at ×0.70 (Ch16, Pale Rim). Contrast bottoms out at 0.25 (Ch16) and peaks at 0.70 (Ch14, the wedding).

## Narrative light rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Rising light = released. Sinking or seaward light = held.** Motes that are released rise in slow vertical spirals (0.15–0.4 m/s upward, a spiral radius of 0.2–0.6 m) and use `glimmer_lift` (`#FFE9B8`), a warm pale gold that brightens to `glimmer_lift_hi` (`#F4F8FF`) as it fades into the sky. Motes that are held drift sideways and down toward water (0.05–0.2 m/s, mostly lateral, with a downward bias of 15–30°) and use `glimmer_held` (`#7FC6D9`), a cool cyan. **Never mix the two hues on one mote.** The transition between them, at a release, is a 1.5 s hue lerp from held to lift that starts as the direction reverses. The player is never told this rule. They learn it, so that the Ebbing (Ch9) looks quietly wrong in hindsight and the Letting (Ch21) looks like the world exhaling.
2. **Bound glimmer** (machines, salt-glass, Lit Age conduits, the Kite's lamps, **and the Vowbound**) uses `glimmer_bound` (`#4FB3B0`). It is steady, never flickers, and never moves on its own: it moves only as part of the thing it is bound into (a housing, a conduit, a Vowbound's form), with no drift, no shed motes, and no trails. Its stillness is its wrongness. **The Vowbound are bound light** ([decisions_log N-05](../story/decisions_log.md)): Ilun in his chamber (`ch03_shrine_int`, haluna.md S08) and every summon (C10) use this treatment, so the player's own summons share the stillness of the Lit Age machines. A Vowbound turns to rising `glimmer_lift` only at its Act III release (Ilun: Ch20), through the rule 1 hue lerp. Rhen's lantern is **not** bound light: it burns oil (`oil_lamp`, [N-02](../story/decisions_log.md)) and does not go dark at the Unbinding.
3. **The night sea-glow.** Every night frame, and every frame where open sea is visible after sunset, has a faint ambient emission on the sea surface and in the water column: `sea_glow` (`#1C6B78`) at energy 0.04–0.15, modulated by slow noise (a period of 8–20 s and an amplitude of ±25%). It is present from the Prologue through `ending_unbinding`, and it is **exactly 0.0** in `ending_dock`. Do not cut it or drop it below 0.04 for performance on any preset. The Ending steps it down to 0.06 (`ending_unbinding`, below) and holds it there until the cut to `ending_dock`. If it costs too much, find a cheaper way to draw it and tell Narrative (bible §11). Its absence in the last shot is the payoff of the entire game.

   **Night value order (M1-ART-04; replaces "the glow is the brightest value").** The v0 wording came from references §1.8, where "the glow" meant glimmer. Applied to sea-glow, it couldn't be met: sea-glow 0.12 × `#1C6B78` is about 0.02–0.04 linear even with the full water column, while moonlit coral is about 0.12–0.17. To make the sea the brightest value, the moon would have to drop below about 0.05, or sea-glow would have to leave its 0.04–0.15 band. Either one turns night stone black, or the sea into the "lit teal floor" glimmer.md warns about, and contradicts "faint". So at night (sun below −6°), on screen, the order is:
   1. Oil flames and glimmer mote cores: the brightest values, as small sources.
   2. Moonlit stone and sand.
   3. **The glowing sea: the brightest water value and the frame's one teal field.**
   4. Sky, then shadow.

   This is checked by three ratios, which hold at any exposure:
   - **(a) Glow on/off ≥ 1.5×.** The sea with the frame's sea-glow must be at least 1.5× the luminance of the same view with sea-glow 0, so the glow is seen rather than inferred. Night frames therefore use **≥ 0.08** (measured 1.3× at 0.04 and 1.9× at 0.12, so 0.08 clears 1.5× by interpolation). The one named exception is `ending_unbinding` E4 0.07 and E5 0.06 (below). It is recorded in palette.json as `sea_glow_night_exceptions` and checked by `tools/tests/test_palette_rules.py` ([ADR 0022](../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)). The 0.04 floor stays for dusk frames where the sun is up and the glow is residual.
   - **(b) Moonlit stone ≤ 2.5× the glowing sea.** The authoring rule that guarantees it is **moon key energy ≤ 2.5 × sea-glow energy**. Rims culled to characters (light layer 2) don't count. `ending_dock` and `post_credits` have no sea-glow by design, so (a)–(c) don't apply there.
   - **(c) Hue and chroma.** The sea reads teal (Oklab hue 190–230°) with chroma ≥ 0.05, above any moonlit stone's. A navy sky may match its chroma, but at a different hue.

   **Practical- and glimmer-keyed night frames (M1-NAR-08).** Ratio (b) is a rule about **moonlight**. Some night frames are keyed by a practical source (oil lamps, fire) or by glimmer instead: Ch4 (lamp key 1.2, sea-glow 0.12), Ch5 (lamp key 1.6, sea-glow 0.08), and `ending_unbinding` (glimmer key 0.8, the sea-glow ramp). In those frames, stone and characters inside a lamp or glimmer pool are legitimately about 5–15× the glowing sea. That pool is tier 1's light falling on things, so it is not a breach of tier 2. For these frames:
   - Surfaces **inside a practical or glimmer pool** are exempt from (b), the same way character rims are. A pool must read as a pool, with a visible falloff to the frame's ambient values. It must not become a frame-wide key.
   - (b) still applies, unchanged, to any moonlit or sky-lit surface outside the pools.
   - (a) and (c) apply unchanged. The sea is still the brightest *water* value and the frame's one teal field. In `ending_unbinding`, (a) is required while the ramp is at or above 0.08 (E1–E3). E4–E5 fall below 0.08 on purpose, because there the glow is fading toward the edge of sight. They must not reach it: E5 holds at 0.06 (interpolated on/off about 1.45×) so that it still reads as "nearly dark, but not yet" and the cut to `ending_dock`'s 0.0 is a visible step ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md); Rendering to confirm when the mood frame is built).
   - So the on-screen order becomes: (1) flames and mote cores; (2) the surfaces they light, inside their pools; (3) moonlit stone outside the pools, where the frame has a moon; (4) the glowing sea; (5) sky, then shadow.

   Measured in the water test scene (`water_night`: moon 0.25, sea-glow 0.12, exposure 1.1): sea `#003845` against `#002734` with glow off (1.9×), stone `#424E5C` (2.3× the sea), sea hue 219° at C 0.057, stone C 0.028. Ch9's moon was 0.6 against sea-glow 0.15 (stone 2.9× the sea), so it is now **0.35** (2.35×).
4. **Oil flame** (`oil_lamp`, ~2,900 K) is the only warm practical light in a world without glimmer-tech, and the last light the game shows.
5. **Shadows are teal, never neutral black.** In daylight, the LUT toe is tinted toward `deep_teal_shadow` (`#0E4A55`). At night it's tinted toward `night_sky` (`#050A14`). No frame's black point is (0, 0, 0).

## Water looks (validated in the water test scene, M1-ART-04)

A **water look** is a zone's water material: absorption, `col_scatter_shallow`, `col_deep` and `col_sss`. Frames pick one and set `absorption_scale` on the shared base σ = (2.0, 0.5, 0.42) m⁻¹, which Rendering validated in M1-RND-01. The looks live in `game/levels/_tests/swatches/water_looks.json`.

**How they were validated.** `water_look_probe.tscn` renders the M1-RND-01 water test scene (`game/levels/_tests/water/water_test.tscn`, unmodified) with the look's material under a representative frame's sun, sky, ambient and exposure. It then takes the **median on-screen color per seabed depth band**, using the depth where each pixel's view ray meets the sea.
- The 0.3–1 m and 1–4 m bands are read from the test scene's `Lagoon` camera (5.5 m up).
- The 4–15 m and > 15 m bands are read from a probe "survey" camera (40 m up, 39° down), so grazing-angle sky reflection doesn't stand in for depth. Grazing views of deep water are always lighter: Fresnel reflects the sky, which is physical.

At Rendering's values, the probe reproduces the `water_shallows` baseline to RMSE 0.004, inside the regression threshold of 0.008. Rerun it to check any change.

**Pass criteria.**
- **Act I clear water** has palette targets. Each band must be within ΔE_ok ≤ 0.07 (Oklab distance) of the ramp swatch's `onscreen_hex` "0": `turquoise_shallow` for 0.3–1 m, `lagoon_teal` for 1–4 m, `reef_blue` for 4–15 m. The > 15 m band is checked against `deep_teal_shadow` "+1", because the sky reflection that a view of deep water always carries adds about a stop.
- **Every other look** must read as its intent at every band: no band milky (OkL > 0.8 at chroma < 0.04, except `pale_rim`, whose intent is near-white), the hue family as stated, and the survey bands darker than the shallow bands.

**Ch2 held motes (Cutscene A, `@vfx glimmer_held at=rhen_shallows count=9 drift=down_seaward`).** These are exact values, inside palette.json's rules:
- **Swatch and energy:** `glimmer_held` `#7FC6D9`, mote-core emission energy **0.6** (the frame's `hymn_glimmer.a`; the field follows scene intensity, `intensity` 1.0). Bloom is the frame's default; there is no extra glow pass.
- **Count:** **exactly 9**, on every quality preset. The field is fixed-count, so the preset `amount_scale` (0.35 on Low) must not apply to it (a request to Rendering). No respawn: the nine spawn once and stay in the scene.
- **Size:** core 0.012–0.030 m (capped under the global `size_max_m` 0.045 so halos stay small at 50 mm).
- **Motion:** held mode, at the slow end: drift 0.05–0.10 m/s, down bias 15–30°, heading seaward (east, azimuth 95°, away from the beach), ±35° spread. They start 0.5–2.0 m from Rhen, at or just under the surface (0–0.15 m deep), and fade by underwater absorption as they sink. No attractors: they do not gather around Rhen or Asha. They are leaving.
- **No fill light** (`fill_light_energy` 0). The motes must not light Rhen. The dawn rim does that.
- **Frame area:** ≤ **1%** of H06 (core plus halo, measured on the swatch-mask view), well under the 3% daytime limit. In H05 they are behind the lens. In H01 they are sub-pixel at 180 m: do not boost them to make them read there.
- **Sea-glow:** 0.04, the palette floor. The old value (0.03) could not render: the sea-glow gate clamps any visible value to 0.04–0.15.
