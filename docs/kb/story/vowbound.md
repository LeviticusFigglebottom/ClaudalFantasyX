# The Vowbound

Seven bound spirits of past Cantors. The Order calls them "the Saints who stayed." The truth is that each is a **displaced heart** of the Undertow: the glimmer of a Cantor pushed out of the storm when the next Cantor sang, caught by the Order, and bound into a temple stone to teach the next verse of the Hush. Fifty-three other displaced hearts were consumed by the Abiding (see [bible §3](bible.md)).

**Gameplay contract (SPEC §6.3):** each Vowbound replaces the whole party when called, and each is fought during the Unbinding dive (Act III, Ch20–21). **Once a Vowbound is released it leaves Asha's roster for good.** The player's toolkit shrinks as the finale advances, which plays the theme (nobody keeps everything) through the mechanics. Combat should make sure the final fights stay winnable without them.

**Shared design language (for Art; full sheets go in `docs/kb/art/vowbound/`):** huge, regal, spectral, and each part animal, part memory of the person. Every one carries **one human keepsake** worked into its form, the thing that anchored it. When it is released in the finale, the keepsake is the last thing to fade.

**Acquisition order:** Ilun (Ch3, Water), Kahre (Ch5, Lightning), Virel (Ch8, Fire), Nhoa (Ch11, Ice), Hesk (Ch12, Holy), Ethren (Ch13, Glimmer), Kesh (Ch16, all six).
**Release order in the dive:** Ilun, Kahre, Virel, Hesk, Nhoa, Ethren, then the Cage-Wardens, then Kesh, then Sefir.

**Why this element order (IP, [ADR 0015](../decisions/0015-vowbound-element-temple-order.md), [decisions_log N-09](decisions_log.md), [N-15](decisions_log.md)):** the first draft ran Water, Fire, Lightning, Glimmer, Ice, Holy, then the edge of the world, which tracked the inspiring game's temple sequence almost step for step. N-09 put Lightning before Fire and Holy in the mountains, but it gave Ice to the Order's own Saint, in a frozen basin at the Order's seat, right before the wedding trap and the heretic turn. That recreated the inspiring game's frozen-lake temple followed by the wedding betrayal. Now Ice sits in the deep cold under Sunken Vey (Ch11), two chapters and a mountain range away from Ansedra, and **no temple anywhere is frozen**. The Order's Saint is **Glimmer**: the showpiece of a church that forbids binding glimmer is made of bound glimmer, which is the Act II truth hanging in plain sight. Do not move Ice to Ch12 or Ch13, and do not put ice, frost or a frozen basin in any temple from Ch12 to Ch15.

## 1. Ilun, the Net-Warden *(Water, vertical slice)*
- **Temple:** the Shell Shrine, on a tidal islet off Haluna.
- **Who he was:** **Teyo Marrin**, the 50th Cantor, sang about 140 years ago. He was a sixteen-year-old Haluna fisher boy and a weak hearer, who volunteered so the Order would not take his younger sister **Pila**, a stronger one. He was terrified the whole pilgrimage and went anyway.
- **Form:** a vast spectral manta ray with a heron's crest. Its wing-membranes are patterned like knotted fishing net in light, and a **child's net-float** of glass hangs from its tail (the keepsake).
- **Verse taught:** the first verse. The Velamir women have hummed it as a lullaby since an earlier Velamir Cantor sang it home from the shrine door. Rhen knows it as his father's lullaby ([N-11](decisions_log.md)).
- **Battle identity:** defensive water magic and a tidal shield. **Swell:** *Floodnet*, a sweeping net of water.
- **The final fight:** the first release, gentle and almost a tutorial in letting go. Ilun keeps putting himself between Asha and the dark even while they fight, out of habit. When he weakens he asks, in a boy's voice, whether Pila had a good life. The vertical slice's optional side scene plants the answer: the Haluna elder **Grandmother Osu** is Pila's granddaughter, and Pila lived to ninety-one. If the player saw the scene, Asha can tell him. If not, she says honestly that she doesn't know, and he lifts anyway. *It lands as: kindness repaid, and the first goodbye that doesn't hurt.*

## 2. Kahre, the Forge-Crowned *(Lightning)*
- **Temple:** Ashcrown, Pemakar.
- **Who he was:** **Daro Pemakan**, the 36th Cantor, sang about 300 years ago. He was a seventy-one-year-old terrace smith, the oldest Cantor on record, who went because the chosen girl died of fever and the Calm was failing. He is gruff and practical, and he resents three centuries of standing still.
- **Form:** a towering hornbill-headed beast with a mane of running sparks and a chest like a struck anvil. Every blow it lands flashes white, and the crack of hammer on anvil comes after, like thunder. **A smith's hammer** is fused into its casque (the keepsake).
- **Battle identity:** raw lightning damage that ignores defence. **Swell:** *Last Strike*, one blow of the hammer that the whole field hears.
- **The final fight:** he fights hard and grins about it. "Finally. Hit harder, I'm old." He has wanted to be done for three hundred years. Mid-fight he speaks to **Kerrid**, the dead to the dead: "It doesn't hurt, son. It's like setting the tools down." *It lands as: relief and permission. Kerrid's own ending is foreshadowed with comfort instead of dread.*

## 3. Virel, the Kite-Serpent *(Fire)*
- **Temple:** Coilspire, on the Corrow headland.
- **Who she was:** **Wenna Corr**, the 53rd Cantor, sang about 95 years ago. A Corrow-born girl of hidden Saltwright blood who passed as Order-faithful her whole life. She scratched tiny line-marks into the stones of every temple she visited, a secret trail for her people that nobody ever found.
- **Form:** a long ribbon-serpent made of sailcloth panels with fire running along every seam, like a Saltwright signal kite flown alight as a flare. **A brass tuning key** hangs at its throat (the keepsake). Its shape is the inspiration Dace used for the Kite's sails (Liss discovers this in Act III).
- **Battle identity:** fast multi-hit fire that delays enemies on the timeline. **Swell:** *Flare Line*, the whole kite-string burning at once.
- **The final fight:** Liss's fight. Liss found Wenna's scratched marks across Act II and knows her story by now. Wenna is giddy and proud to meet a wright, and she asks what they've built since. Liss names the Kite. *It lands as: Liss getting her people's lost history back and giving Wenna the recognition she never had, moments before Liss must give up her own machines.*

## 4. Hesk, the Cord-Keeper *(Holy)*
- **Temple:** Cloudloom, the Ullar Heights.
- **Who she was:** **Ama Quillo**, the 47th Cantor, sang about 175 years ago. She was a thirty-four-year-old Ullari keth-reader and a devout believer who, on pilgrimage, started *counting*: Cantors, Saints, temples. She recorded the arithmetic in keth and hung them among the thousands of cords in Cloudloom's open hall, where nobody thought to read them. *Forty-seven voices. Seven houses. Where did the rest go?* She sang anyway, believing faith could hold a question. Her lone question-knot at the end of the count is the Eighth Verse's place: the count of the verses is eight, not seven (act3_threads T-04).
- **Form:** a great condor whose wing feathers are knotted cords, patterned like Ullari weaving and lit from inside with the clean white light of the high passes. **A keth bundle** is clutched in one talon (the keepsake).
- **Battle identity:** Holy damage, cleansing (she lifts statuses off the party, the way the high passes lift the dead), and Regen. Her light is the truth held up to be read. **Swell:** *Pass-Cord*, a line of shining knots across the field.
- **The final fight:** Toma's fight. Ama was what Toma tried to be, faithful enough to ask. She asks whether anyone read her cords. Toma says he did, and that they broke his faith, and thanks her for it. *It lands as: a believer honouring the believer who told the truth.*

## 5. Nhoa, the Rootlight *(Ice)*
- **Temple:** Lowlight, Sunken Vey. Its inner sanctum lies underwater at the foot of a Lit Age well-shaft, where cold water from the open deep wells up through the floor. Down there the light is low, the water is the coldest in Maresh, and drowned glimmer sifts down through it like silt. Nothing in the temple is frozen: the cold is the deep's.
- **Who she was:** **Ressa**, the 56th Cantor, sang about 70 years ago. She was the only Kirru Cantor and Pell's clutch-sister, and she was a hearer who believed she could end the storm from inside.
- **Form:** a tall Kirru-like figure made entirely of drifting motes that sift around her like silt in deep water, with antlers of mangrove root and a long heron neck. She moves through water as if it were air, and the cold moves with her. **A name-shell** (her own, carved) hangs at her throat (the keepsake).
- **Battle identity:** Ice damage carried on sifting mote-swarms, and the ability to *copy* the last enemy action (an echo of Pell). **Swell:** *Farewell-Tide*, the settling silt turning and rising in a column of light (the only Vowbound finisher where the motes go *up*, a visual seed for the Letting).
- **The final fight:** Pell's fight. She is glad to see him and sad that he is old. He fights her silently. When she starts to lift, he **turns his back**, the Kirru farewell he refused her seventy years ago, and sings. *It lands as: the most complete act of love in the game, done by looking away.*

## 6. Ethren, the Level Saint *(Glimmer)*
- **Temple:** the Sanctum of the Level, Ansedra. Its inner basin is water held so still, by the machines under the city, that it is a perfect mirror, and it is lit from beneath by a steady light that never moves. Pilgrims call it a miracle: water that has not stirred in four hundred years. It is the first sight of what stillness costs, and the light under it is the same bound glimmer the Order burns Saltwright camps for (Ch6).
- **Who he was:** **Corran Ysel**, the 60th Cantor, sang 24 years ago. He was a thirty-one-year-old Ansedran Tender and Ysolde's husband: patient, funny, and a terrible cook. He was displaced when Imra sang eleven years ago and placed in the Sanctum, the Order's showpiece Saint.
- **Form:** the Order's ideal made literal: an armoured winged figure of gold lacquer with a veiled face and a bowl-halo behind its head, its gold lit from inside by a steady bound light that never drifts. It is beautiful and impersonal. **A plain copper wedding ring** sits on one gauntleted finger (the keepsake), the only human thing about it, and the only part of it that doesn't shine.
- **Battle identity:** Glimmer damage and stillness: Slow and Stop on enemies, and mass wards on the party (Combat coins the ward names; never the series' spell names). **Swell:** *Still Water*, the Order's whole creed in one blow: everything on the field stops.
- **The final fight:** Ysolde's fight, and the hardest Vowbound fight in the game. Mid-fight the veil falls, and it is just Corran's face. He tells her he heard every visit. She tells him she is not coming back to the Sanctum anymore. *It lands as: the widow's goodbye she has been refusing for twenty-four years.* The ring is the last thing to fade.

## 7. Kesh, the First Voice *(all six elements in turn)*
- **Resting place:** the Drowned Choir, bound in the stones by Sefir himself.
- **Who she was:** **Kesh Aurane**, the 1st Cantor and a Lamplighter. Sefir's wife. She sang herself in a thousand years ago and was displaced forty years later. She has been awake in stone ever since, unable to lift, while her husband became what he became. She hid the Eighth Verse in the Choir stones: its Lamptongue words cut plain, and its melody in fragments that can only be read through the Kirru farewell-tide (act3_threads T-04, the Lead 2026-10-30).
- **Form:** a colossal woman of standing seawater, rising from the Choir's sunken bowl. Her robe is a hundred open choir-mouths. **A Lamplighter's lamp-key** hangs at her belt (the keepsake).
- **Battle identity:** she cycles through all six elements by phase (a Combat note: telegraph each shift on the timeline). **Swell:** *The Seven*, all seven verses at once.
- **Acquisition:** binding her at the Choir is what shows Asha, through Kesh's memory, the truth of the Hush: the singer becomes the storm's next heart. The founding itself is already known from the Lamp Archive (Ch13, [ADR 0016](../decisions/0016-act-two-restaging.md)).
- **Release:** in the heart (Ch21 H03) Asha calls her out of the sleeve one last time; she is released like the six before her (act3_threads T-01).
- **The final fight:** fought just before Sefir, with Sefir watching. She fights at full strength because she *wants* the party strong enough to beat him. When she lifts, she doesn't look at him. *It lands as: a thousand-year-old woman choosing release over the man who wouldn't let her go, which is the thesis of the game, shown to its antagonist.*
