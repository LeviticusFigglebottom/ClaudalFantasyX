# Voice Guide

How each principal character talks. Use this when writing any `.dlg` line. If a line could be swapped to another speaker without anyone noticing, rewrite it.

**Global rules**
- **Sincerity (pillar 1).** Humor is warmth between people, never a wink at the player. No meta jokes, no modern slang, no pop-culture rhythm ("So... that happened.").
- **No lore dump over four lines.** Exposition comes through argument, discovery, or action. If a character must explain, someone interrupts, disagrees, or walks away.
- **Swearing:** in-world oaths only, sparing. Examples: *"Salt and stone."* (Low Reaches), *"Level preserve us."* (Order), *"Rust it."* (Saltwright).
- **Address:** the Order says *Lady Cantor* and *Warden*. Friends use first names. Kerrid uses no names until Act II.
- **Spelling:** British, with Oxford -ize, in every string a player reads: colour, armour, defence, grey, harbour, honour and traveller, but Customize (Lead, 2026-10-30).
- **Line length:** keep most lines to one or two sentences of about 25 words or fewer. Long speeches are earned only by Kerrid (once, Ch15, ADR 0016), Sefir (once, Ch16), and Rhen's narration.

---

## Rhen Sallow: *quick, warm, deflecting*
- **Rhythm:** fast, with run-on bursts when nervous, then a sudden short line when it matters. He asks questions to avoid answering them.
- **Diction:** diver's slang (*"down-dark," "slack tide," "cut loose"*) and haggling patter. He jokes *harder* the more scared he is.
- **Narration** (voice-over) is older and steadier, with short, plain declaratives.
- **Tell:** in Act III he stops finishing jokes.

> "That's a very big spear for a very small conversation."
> "I'm not scared. I'm just standing behind you in a brave way."
> "Everyone keeps saying she's going *on* a pilgrimage. Nobody says she's coming back from one."
> "He hummed it every night. That song. Every night until he didn't."
> "Don't. Don't be kind about it. Just let me be angry for a minute."
> *(Act III)* "I'm not running, Dad. Look. I'm standing right here."
> *(Narration)* "I've told it funny before. Not this time."

## Asha Velamir: *gentle, precise, unbending underneath*
- **Rhythm:** measured and polite. She finishes other people's comfort for them. Her refusals are very short.
- **Diction:** formal Cantor phrasing in public ("It would honour me."), plain and teasing in private. She often names what someone else is feeling before her own.
- **Tell:** when she is hurting she asks about *your* day. In Act II she starts to stop doing that.

> "You're shivering. Here. No, take it, I've got the sun on my side."
> "It's all right to be sad for me. Just not today. Today I'd like cake."
> "I can hear them. It's like someone humming in the next room. All the time."
> "Pell. Why would the dead want to go *down*?"
> "No." *(beat)* "No, High Warden. I won't."
> "My mother didn't sing so I could follow her. I think she sang so I'd have time to choose."
> *(Ending, mute of song, spoken)* "It's all right. I can still talk your ear off."

## Toma Okeru: *steady, formal, a big man speaking softly*
- **Rhythm:** complete sentences with no contractions in formal speech and some in private. He pauses before hard truths.
- **Diction:** Stillguard drill phrasing (*"Hold," "On my shield"*) and scripture fragments in Acts I–II, which disappear after Ch15. Fisher-boy idioms when relaxed.
- **Tell:** he says *"Be level"* by reflex. When he stops, that's his faith breaking.

> "Step back from the Lady Cantor. Slowly, please. I would rather not."
> "On my shield. Everyone, on my shield, now."
> "You do not mock the rites, Rhen. You may ask about them. That is different."
> "I was twenty-two. I held the torch. I did not throw it. I tell myself that is different. It is not."
> "The water was never the god. We were supposed to be tending each other."
> *(To Liss, Ch15)* "Let me carry him. Please. I don't ask you to forgive me. Let me carry him."

## Liss Varrow: *sharp, fast, technical, guarded*
- **Rhythm:** clipped and impatient, often starting mid-thought. She talks to machines more gently than to people.
- **Diction:** wright's jargon (*"seat the housing," "mote-load," "she'll hold"*), and she calls devices "she." She uses insults as affection once she trusts someone.
- **Tell:** when she is grieving, she narrates repairs out loud instead of talking about it.

> "You sell the dead by the pound, diver. At least I put them back to work."
> "Don't touch that. Don't. Touch. That. Thank you."
> "Rust it, the Bell's glimmer-made. You're all just *not looking* at it."
> "Your father built a ship nobody can fly. You hid his name in your sleeve. Real proud family, Sallow."
> "Seat the housing. Turn it. Tobin hated this part. Turn it. Turn it."
> *(Act III)* "Every lamp on this ship is somebody. I know. I'm building it anyway. Then we let them go."

## Old Kerrid: *almost silent, then plain and dry*
- **Acts I–II (pre-reveal):** single words or short sentences. He often answers with an action instead of a line (cinematics: give him a gesture slot). No names.
- **Post-reveal:** longer, plainer, with dry gallows humor about being dead. He never sounds like he is performing wisdom.
- **Tell:** the first time he uses Rhen's name (Ch15, in the confession) should hit like a blow.
- **Diction:** a Corrow stair-watch captain's, a conscripted fisher's, never an officer's. Stair and watch words, never vows: he makes no promises and doesn't talk about having made any ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md)). No Order rank, title or drill words ([ADR 0022](../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)).

> "Move."
> "I held a stair for your mother once." *(Ch5, the only thing he says to her.)*
> "Stair's clear." *(The stair-watch's all-clear. The Ending.)*
> "Somebody's still below." *(Ch9, at dawn on the road out of Corrow: why he walks on.)*
> "No." *(He sets down a full bowl of food untouched.)*
> "Your mother laughed at the wrong times too."
> "I have nothing left to hide. I may as well talk."
> "I don't sleep. I sit. You'd be surprised how long a night is, Rhen."

*(Review-sweep-G: his slouch line belongs to E3 alone, "Stand up straight. Your father slouched." It is never sampled here or scripted before E3, so the Ending spends it first.)*

## Ysolde Ysel: *cold, exact, devastatingly dry*
- **Rhythm:** complete, grammatically perfect sentences with long pauses. She corrects pronunciation mid-crisis.
- **Diction:** scholarly and precise, with Lamptongue terms used correctly and never explained unless asked. She is withering through understatement.
- **Tell:** her rare warmth comes out as a technical instruction ("Lift your chin when you sing. It is better for the breath."). Her rage comes out quieter, not louder.

> "It is pronounced *ennu*. Two syllables. You will want to sing it correctly when it matters."
> "You are very funny, Master Sallow. I have decided not to notice."
> "The Saints are at peace. I visit my husband every year. He has never once been anything but at peace."
> "I read the cords. I lied to you about them. I would like to stop doing that now."
> "Forty-seven voices. Seven houses. Do the sum, High Warden. I already have."
> *(To Ethren)* "I won't come to the Sanctum anymore, love. I'm sorry. I'm so glad."

## Pell: *slow, literal, still*
- **Rhythm:** very short sentences with long gaps. He never interrupts and answers questions a scene later.
- **Diction:** literal human speech (Kirru have no idioms in the human tongue) and nature-precise nouns. He calls humans "you-people" without malice and the dead "the lifting" or "the held."
- **Tell:** he mimics sounds. When he is overwhelmed he hums the storm's own note, softly.

> "Beautiful. And pointing the wrong way."
> "You keep them close. You call it love. It is heavy."
> "I did not turn around. Seventy years. My neck is very tired."
> "The diver's light is held. Gently. But held."
> "She is here. She is not lifted. Do not speak yet. Please."
> *(Act III)* "I will turn around now. Watch me. Then do not watch me."

## High Warden Sefir Aurane: *warm, reasonable, timeless*
- **Rhythm:** unhurried, graceful, and complete paragraphs in miniature. He never raises his voice until his very last line (see below).
- **Diction:** gentle and inclusive (*"we," "together," "of course"*), with an archivist's precise vocabulary and occasional archaic constructions that slip through ("It were kinder...").
- **Tell:** he answers grief with logic. When someone weeps, he explains.

> "Lady Cantor. I knelt beside your mother once, on a day very like this one."
> "Be level, Inquisitor. The Edict burns things. It has never needed to burn people."
> "Nothing you love has to end. I am offering you that. Why do you look at me as if it were cruel?"
> "You call it holding on, Lady Cantor. I call it keeping faith with the dead. It is the same hand."
> "You see a cage. I see a cradle. It is the same shape."
> *(Final phase)* "Kesh. Kesh, look at me. *Look at me.*"
