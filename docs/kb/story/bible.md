# Story Bible: *Hymn of the Undertow*

Owner: Narrative Director. Status: M1 expansion of SPEC §3. Canon unless superseded by an ADR.
Related: [act outline](beats/act_outline.md), [Vowbound](vowbound.md), [voice guide](voice_guide.md), [characters](characters/), [new glossary terms](glossary_additions.md).

This document expands the §3 seed. The premise, themes, and ending shape are unchanged. Where it adds detail, the detail serves pillar 6: *breaking a cycle of sacrifice is right, and nobody gets to keep everything.*

---

## 1. The Core Idea in Three Sentences

The dead of Maresh are not let go. Their light sinks into the sea, pools, and becomes the Undertow. A thousand years ago, a man who could not let his wife go built a faith to keep that storm asleep, and he has fed it a singer's life every generation since.

**Thematic engine:** every culture, rite, and character in this bible answers one question: *what do you do with grief?* The Order keeps grief still. The Saltwrights put it to work. The Kirru let it go. The party has to learn the Kirru answer, and it costs them.

---

## 2. Glimmer: The Metaphysics

- **The lamp.** Every living person carries glimmer, which people call their *lamp*. "Her lamp went out" means she died. Animals carry faint lamps. Plants carry none.
- **Release.** At death, the lamp breaks into **motes**: drifting, pale-gold to blue-white specks of light. Left alone, motes **lift**. They rise slowly over one night and fade into the stars. This is the natural, healthy end.
- **Grief anchors.** Motes respond to the living. When mourners hold on (refusing the death, hoarding keepsakes, swearing they will never forget), the motes hang near them. Anchored motes grow heavy and drift *down*. Water, and salt water especially, draws them. Over centuries the sea of Maresh has grown thick with the dead. **The sea glows faintly at night everywhere**, and has done so for a thousand years. People think this is simply what the sea looks like.
- **Binding.** The ancients learned to trap motes in salt-glass and ceramic housings. Bound glimmer gives steady light and motive force. A bound mote never lifts. Every ancient lamp is a person who never got to leave.
- **The unrisen.** Rarely, a dying person's will is so fixed on something unfinished (a promise, a duty, a watch not yet over) that their motes cohere back into their own shape ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md)). The result is one of the **unrisen**: a dead person who walks, talks, and remembers. They don't eat, breathe, bleed, or age, and their breath never fogs in the cold. Wounds on them leak motes instead of blood. They are not monsters, but they are slowly fraying, and each one is something that has not been finished.
- **The Undertow.** Enough anchored glimmer, pooled in water and stirred by unresolved grief, becomes a storm with a will. It is not malicious. It is the *want* of the dead: to be held, to be remembered, to drag the living close. It drowns coastal towns because it reaches for people.
- **The heart.** The storm only holds together, and sleeps, when it has a coherent voice at its center. The Hush gives it one (see §6).
- **What would really end it:** lift the dead. If every mote in the sea, the machines, and the storm were released together, the Undertow would have nothing left to be. That is the Great Unbinding.

**Visual rule for Art and VFX:** motes that *lift* rise in slow vertical spirals and fade upward. Motes that are *anchored* drift sideways and down, toward water. Players should learn this visual language by Act II without being told, so that the Order's seaward dance looks quietly wrong in hindsight.

## 3. History and the Cycle of Calms

Dates are counted **AL ("after the Level")**, the Order's calendar, which starts from the founding of the faith. The present day is **1012 AL**, the end of the **61st Calm**.

| Era | Events |
|---|---|
| **The Lit Age** (c. 400 years before AL) | Maresh's great cities, led by **Iskarra**, run on bound glimmer: lamp-towers, tide engines, skyships. Mourning becomes a civic virtue. The dead are bound into city lights "so they may keep watching over us." The sea rises slowly. Nobody connects the two. |
| **The First Rising** (0 AL) | The first Undertow drowns Iskarra and most of the old coasts in a single season. |
| **The Founding** (0–4 AL) | Six Iskarran **Lamplighters** (lamp-engineers and singers) compose the **Hush**, a song structure that coheres glimmer around a willing voice. At the Drowned Choir, the singer **Kesh** sings herself into the storm's center as its first heart. Four other Lamplighters bind themselves around her as the **Cage**. The sixth, **Sefir Aurane**, Kesh's husband, stays outside to keep watch. He founds the **Order of the Still Water** and writes its doctrine. |
| **The First Displacement** (c. 41 AL) | The storm stirs, and a second Cantor sings. Her arrival pushes Kesh's spent glimmer out of the heart. Sefir can't bear to let Kesh lift, so he binds her in the Choir's stones. While he does it, he learns that drawing on a displaced heart keeps his own lamp burning. **The Abiding** begin that night. |
| **The Salt Edicts** (212 AL) | The Order outlaws binding and all salvage of Lit Age devices. Iskarran engineer families who refuse become the **Saltwrights**. |
| **The Shortening** (c. 600 AL–present) | Calms that once lasted 30–40 years now last 11–15. The Abiding have grown in number and draw more. The Order preaches that the shortening proves the world's debt is deepening. |
| **The 60th Cantor** (988 AL) | **Corran Ysel**, Ysolde's husband, sings. That Calm lasts 13 years. |
| **The 61st Cantor** (1001 AL) | **Imra Velamir**, Asha's mother, sings. On her pilgrimage the Undertow strikes Corrow. Imra goes down the Fisher Stair into the sea for a drowning diver she has never met: **Dace Sallow** (Rhen's father), there on wall-work. **Kerrid**, captain of the stair-watch, holds the stair open, gets them both up it, and is taken by the sea at its foot; he rises unrisen at the next low tide ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md), [ADR 0022](../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)). Dace was never her guardian and never walks her road. He carries her comb home to Haluna, begins a hull at Hollowmere, and after Imra sings he sails into the Undertow after her, to repay the debt, and is never seen again. He never goes home to Oruvel: the sea tore his sleeves on the stair, Imra's escort saw his line-marks, and a hunted Saltwright going home would bring the Order to his son. His one message asks **Mother Kell** to keep Rhen, and she does (ADR 0022's sweep-G note). Corran is displaced and bound at Ansedra as the Vowbound Ethren. |
| **The Present** (1012 AL) | The Undertow rises after only 11 years. **Asha Velamir** is named the 62nd Cantor. |

**How Vowbound are made (and what the Order hides):** each time a new Cantor sings, the old heart is pushed out. The Order catches that displaced glimmer. A few of the strongest are bound into temples as **Vowbound**, which the Order calls "the Saints who stayed." Each one teaches the next Cantor a verse. A temple holds only one Vowbound. When a new one is placed, the old one is "retired": the Abiding consume it. Of 60 displaced hearts, seven survive (see [vowbound.md](vowbound.md)).

## 4. The Order of the Still Water

**Creed (public):** *The Drowning is debt.* The Lit Age bound the dead and the sea rose to collect, so the Undertow is the world's punishment. Stillness is atonement: build nothing that binds, keep no lamps but oil, mourn quietly, and send the dead to the sea. When the debt is paid, the **Last Calm** will come and the Undertow will sleep forever.

**Creed (true, known only to the Abiding):** the storm can't be ended, only kept. Keeping it requires a heart every generation, and a full reservoir so the Abiding may live. The Order forbids binding so that *only the Order binds*.

**Symbol: the Level.** A circle (a bowl seen from above) crossed by one horizontal line (still water). The gesture is both palms held flat, face down, lowered slowly, "settling the water." The greeting is *"Be level."* and the reply is *"And still."*

### Structure

| Rank | Role |
|---|---|
| **High Warden** | Head of the Order. Sefir Aurane has held the seat under many names: he "retires" and returns as his own "successor" every few decades, behind veils and new titles. |
| **The Abiding Conclave** | Five revenants (Sefir, plus Wardens **Hesper Oan**, **Malcor Venn**, **Ilsabet Rue**, and **Old Tamsin Druse**). They are the only ones who know the truth. Publicly they are "the Conclave of Elders." |
| **Wardens** | Regional governors of the faith, one per region. Most are living, sincere, and ignorant of the truth. |
| **Tenders** | Temple priests. They keep the temples, tend the Vowbound, and run the trials. |
| **Ebb-priests** | Perform small Ebbings for ordinary deaths. A Cantor performs the great Ebbing after an Undertow attack. |
| **The Stillguard** | Temple warriors in red-lacquered shell armor with round shields. They guard temples and Cantors, and they enforce the Salt Edicts. Toma was one. |
| **Reeds** | Novices. They take the name because a reed "bends and does not break the water." |

### Rites

- **The Ebbing** (dance for the dead). After a death or a drowning, a priest (or a Cantor, for mass deaths) dances on a floating platform with a long **lantern-staff**, drawing the motes after her and out to sea. Mourners kneel facing the water. It is beautiful. *It is also the reason the Undertow exists:* the Ebbing herds the dead into the sea instead of letting them lift. The Order designed the steps that way.
- **The Stilling.** A rite performed on the unrisen. Stillguard chant in a ring and press with flat palms, and the unrisen's motes are torn loose and drawn seaward. The Order calls it mercy. It is painful.
- **The Pilgrim's Wake.** The night before a Cantor departs, her village holds a funeral for her while she is still alive: food, songs, and her name carved on a shell and set in the shrine wall. The Cantor attends her own wake. (This is the source of the Act I side scene at Haluna.)
- **Lampfast.** On the eve of each Calm, all lamps are put out across Maresh and everyone sits in the dark. The only light is the sea glowing.
- **The Vigil of the Calm.** The day the Cantor sings, there is a festival everywhere: kites (cloth only), music, and weddings. Weddings on Vigil day are thought luckiest.
- **Leaf-boats.** Folded palm-leaf offering trays with a pinch of rice and a lit wick, floated for the dead. This is a folk practice the Order absorbed.

**Scripture:** *The Canticle of Level Water*, short verses recited, never sung. Singing in the Order is reserved for Cantors. Ordinary worshippers *hum*, and congregational humming is the Order's sound (a note for Audio).

## 5. Regions and Cultures

Each culture is a **new synthesis**. The real-world influences guide material, silhouette, and texture only. No real sacred site is copied: temples are original layouts built around their own ideas.

### 5.1 The Low Reaches (southwest archipelago)
*Influences: Okinawan/Ryukyuan coastal villages and Polynesian navigation and tattoo traditions.*
Low coral islands, turquoise flats, and constant trade winds. Villages sit behind curved **tide-walls**: dry-stacked coral block with wind-gaps, so that the wall "breathes" rather than breaks. Houses are low, with red clay tile roofs mortared white. The roof ridge carries a carved **lintel-gull**, a wooden seabird facing the sea, said to cry warning before the Undertow. Families navigate by **star-paths**, chanted sequences of stars taught to children as lullabies. Adults wear **reach-bands**: tattooed bands on the forearms, one per voyage survived, so a Low Reaches elder's arms are a record of their life at sea. Food is sea grape, purple yam, smoked bonito, and sweet brown sugar cake. Reefball started here. **Sea-law:** no hull drowned in a Rising is passed at sea without someone going aboard to bring its **name-board** home to its harbour, where it is hung with the wake-shells ([ADR 0017](../decisions/0017-act-one-restaging.md)).
- **Haluna** *(vertical slice)*: a fishing village on a crescent beach with a tide-walled lane, a tiny Order chapel, and the **Shell Shrine** on a tidal islet reached by the Saltgrass Causeway at low tide. Asha's home.
- **Oruvel**: Rhen's home. A raft-town of lashed hulls moored above a drowned Lit Age district of coral-crusted concrete towers. Its people are **lamp-divers**, who go down with oil lanterns to salvage pre-Edict metal and glass.

### 5.2 The Pemakar Terraces (central volcanic isles)
*Influences: Balinese and Javanese temple architecture and Thai/Burmese lacquer and gold leaf.*
Steep green volcanoes cut into rice and taro terraces, with water channels running down every slope. Temples are **stair-mountains**: stepped, tiered platforms of dark volcanic stone that climb the hillside itself, each tier smaller than the last, crowned by a lacquered red pavilion. The gateways are **open-hand gates**, two carved stone forearms rising from the ground with the palms cupped toward each other. Pilgrims walk through the "held" space between them. Every household keeps a small lacquer shrine-box finished in black and red with gold-leaf figures. Terrace cemeteries sit beside the paddies, so festivals happen among graves (pillar 2).
- **Pemakar town**: market terraces, smithies, and lacquer workshops. It hosts **Lanternfall**, a festival where a thousand leaf-boats are floated down the terrace channels at night, so the whole mountain becomes a waterfall of small fires.
- **Ashcrown**: the Lightning temple, a stair-mountain built into the lip of a caldera that still breathes ash. Dry storms crackle in the plume, and Lit Age copper veins the tiers, so the strikes run down the mountain like water down the terraces.

### 5.3 Tamsule (trade port, crossroads)
*Influences: a mix of all the southern cultures, with Burmese/Thai gilded signboards.*
A stilt city on a river mouth, with gilded lacquer shop-signs, floating markets, and a hundred languages. Its center is the **Tamsule Bell**, a colossal Lit Age bronze-and-salt-glass dome that holds a sphere of seawater suspended in the air. Reefball is played inside it. The Order "sanctified" the Bell centuries ago instead of destroying it. That makes it the most visible piece of working glimmer-tech in the world, and the first clue that the Order's law is selective.

### 5.4 Corrow (the seawall coast)
*Influences: Khmer stone masonry at military scale.*
A great curved seawall of grey laterite blocks, carved with rows of faces looking out to sea (the **Watchers**: original carved figures with closed eyes and open mouths, as if humming). Corrow Bastion is a garrison town of Stillguard and conscripted fishers. It has no weapon against the Undertow and has never pretended to: its defence for nine hundred years has been the Coilspire beacon and the **Fisher Stair**, a broad stone stair cut up the wall's inner face so the low town can climb onto the wall ahead of the sea. The wall has been rebuilt after every Rising ([ADR 0017](../decisions/0017-act-one-restaging.md)). The Stillguard garrison holds the Bastion and the wall-head. The stair is kept by the **stair-watch**: conscripted fishers of the low town under a captain chosen from among them, with no rank in the Order. The watch's stair-law is **"First down the stair, last up it"**: a captain doesn't come off the wall while anyone is still below, and the all-clear is *"Stair's clear."* In 1001 AL the watch's captain, Kerrid, died at the stair's foot holding its hand-line. The watch still tells the story at Corrow, and nobody else does ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md), [ADR 0022](../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)).
- **Coilspire**: the Fire temple, a hollow stone spire on the headland where storms always break. Corrow has kept a warning beacon burning at its top for nine hundred years, and the old copper flues coiled through its walls still draw the flame.

### 5.5 Ossa Fen (Kirru estuary)
*Influences: original, a mangrove and reed-delta people.*
Mangrove channels, tannin-dark water, and white egrets. See §8.
- **Reedhollow**: a Kirru roost-village of woven reed lodges raised on mangrove roots.

### 5.6 Sunken Vey (the great drowned city)
*Influences: Khmer stone ruins and Lit Age glimmer architecture.*
The largest Lit Age ruin still above water. Towered stone galleries stand chest-deep in a turquoise lagoon, wrapped in strangler-fig roots, and ancient ceramic conduits still faintly pulse with bound light. Silent, gorgeous, and dead.
- **Lowlight**: the Ice temple, a half-flooded gallery temple whose inner sanctum is entirely underwater, at the foot of a Lit Age well-shaft where cold water from the open deep wells up through the floor. Drowned glimmer sifts down through it like silt. Nothing there is frozen; the cold is the deep's ([N-15](decisions_log.md)).

### 5.7 The Ullar Heights (cold northern ranges)
*Influences: Andean high-altitude culture.*
Thin air, enormous skies, terraced high valleys, and mortarless fitted stone walls of many-sided blocks. The Ullari herd **woolbacks** (long-necked shaggy pack animals) and wear layered hand-woven cloth in dense patterns, many of them *tide patterns*, oddly, so far from the sea. They keep records on **keth**: knotted cord bundles read by touch. Their custom for the dead is to tie a knot for the person on the **pass-cords** strung across every mountain pass, and then *walk on without looking back*. Up here the dead lift quickly. The Ullari are stoic about death in a way the coast finds cold.
- **Quellan**: a highland town of stone and woven awnings.
- **Cloudloom**: the Holy temple, a weaving-hall cut into the summit rock above Quellan, its roof open to the sky and hung with thousands of keth that stir in the thin wind. At noon the high sun comes straight down through the open roof, and the cords cast their shadows on the white stone floor like writing. It is cold, as the whole range is, but it is stone and cord and sky, not ice ([N-15](decisions_log.md)).

### 5.8 Ansedra (the holy city)
*Influences: Thai and Burmese lacquer and gold leaf, plus Javanese tiered roofs, combined into an original city plan.*
The Order's seat, built on a lagoon kept perfectly flat. No wind ripples it, and no wake is permitted (the stillness is maintained by forbidden glimmer machinery under the city, which the party learns of from the Lamp Archive's ledgers in Act II). Its skyline is **bowl-domes**: inverted gilded bowls on tiered red-lacquer bases, each crowned with a thin spire. Barges are poled, never rowed, so the water stays still. The city has no seabirds, no dogs, and no children's games in the squares. The **Lacquer Court** is the ceremonial heart. The **Sanctum of the Level** is the Glimmer temple: its inner basin is held so still that it is a perfect mirror, lit from beneath by a steady light that never moves, and the Order calls it a miracle. The light is bound glimmer, the very thing the Order forbids ([N-15](decisions_log.md)). Below it all lie the **Underreach** and the **Cistern**, the reservoir where *spent* Saints are consumed. The party never goes down there: they read about it in the **Lamp Archive**, the Order's library of Lit Age records, where the founders' ledgers and the full Cantor roll are kept in plain sight ([ADR 0016](../decisions/0016-act-two-restaging.md)).

### 5.9 Hollowmere (the Saltwright flotilla)
See §7.

### 5.10 Iskarra and the Pale Rim
- **Iskarra**: the Lit Age capital, drowned to its sixth storey. White ceramic towers, dead lamp-columns, and plazas under three metres of clear water. This is where the Prologue is set.
- **The Pale Rim**: the southern edge of the known sea. The water is ankle-to-waist deep over white sand for days of travel, and the horizon goes white. At its end is the **Drowned Choir**: a ring of colossal stone figures standing in the water, mouths open, around a sunken amphitheatre. This is where every Cantor ends.

## 6. The Hush and the Cantor Lineage

- **The Hush** is a song of **seven verses**, one learned at each Vowbound's resting place (six temples and the Drowned Choir). It is sung in **Lamptongue**, the old Iskarran liturgical language. Sung whole, at the Choir, by a voice that can *hear* glimmer, it pulls the singer's lamp into the storm's center and makes it the new heart.
- **The Eighth Verse.** The Lamplighters wrote an eighth verse, the **Letting**, which lifts glimmer instead of gathering it. Sefir struck it out, and the Order teaches that the Hush has seven. Kesh hid its melody in the Choir stones. The Kirru farewell song carries it unknowingly, and Ama Quillo's keth record that it existed. Asha sings it at the Great Unbinding, and it costs her singing voice.
- **Hearers.** Cantors are chosen from people who *hear* glimmer, as a faint hum near motes. Hearing runs in families, and the Order tracks those families quietly. The Velamir line of Haluna has given four Cantors, Imra the most recent. Asha has heard the dead since she was four.
- **The Velamir lullaby.** An earlier Velamir Cantor sang the first verse toward home from the Shell Shrine door, and the women of the line have hummed its tune to their children ever since, without the words (only Cantors sing). Imra hummed it long before she was named. **Dace Sallow** heard her hum it on Haluna's dock when he was a young wright trading salvage along the Low Reaches, years before Rhen was born. He never learned her name, and he took the tune home to Oruvel as his son's lullaby. In 1001 he heard it again on the Corrow stair, hummed by the Cantor who had just pulled him out of the sea, and that is how he knew her ([ADR 0022](../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)). Neither child knows the other was sung to sleep with it ([decisions_log N-11](decisions_log.md)).
- **Training.** A named Cantor is given a Lamptongue tutor (Ysolde), at least one sworn guardian (Toma), and a pilgrim's route set by the Order. She may take on any guardians she chooses, which is the custom that lets Rhen join.
- **Lamptongue sound palette** (for Audio's constructed lyrics): open vowels, few hard stops, and roots that recur. *vey* water, *ish* light, *karra* gathering/city, *hal* shell, *ennu* sleep/hush, *mir* stay, *tash* let go, *re* again, *oan* home. The seven verses end on *mir* (stay). The Letting ends on *tash*.

## 7. The Saltwrights

- **Origin:** descendants of Iskarran engineer guilds who refused the Salt Edicts. Their name comes from an old insult ("salt-wrights," people who build with brine-glass) that they now wear proudly.
- **Life:** nomadic flotillas of lashed hulls, some incorporating Lit Age hull plating, with windmills and tide-wheels. The largest is **Hollowmere**, a raft-city of some four thousand people that moves seasonally among the outer shoals to stay ahead of Order patrols.
- **Belief:** *The dead want to be useful.* Saltwrights salvage bound glimmer and put it back to work: water pumps, signal lamps, medicine-warmers. They see this as respect, not theft. **Their blind spot:** they are hoarding the dead too. The story does not make them villains, but the ending costs them every machine they own.
- **Marks:** **line-marks**, tattoos in fine parallel lines and node-dots that look like circuitry. The tradition is a synthesis of Polynesian tattoo practice and Lit Age schematics, and each mark records a device the wearer has restored.
- **Persecution:** Order raids, the burning of salvage camps, and **brine-branding** (a salt-burn across the line-marks). Saltwrights may not enter temples or own land.
- **Navigation:** *current-songs*, sung charts of sea currents that are cousins of the Low Reaches star-paths. This is why Dace Sallow could marry into Oruvel easily: the two peoples share a musical way of knowing the sea.

## 8. The Kirru

- **Who they are:** a non-human people, 2.1–2.4 m tall and lean. Sleek dark fur on the back and shoulders, a pale throat, a long neck and long narrow face with whiskers, a crest of fine heron-like feathers, and long webbed fingers. They live about 250 years, swim superbly, and stand very still for a long time.
- **Speech:** soft, unhurried, and fond of silence. Among themselves they use whistle-clicks and hand-shapes. Most learn the human tongue but speak it sparingly and literally.
- **Grief:** the Kirru do not keep graves or keepsakes. When one dies, the clutch gathers at the tideline at dusk and sings the **farewell-tide**, a wordless rising song, and then they *turn their backs* to the water so the dead are not anchored by being watched. Kirru motes lift within the hour. **This is the key to the whole story.** The Kirru have done right by their dead for a thousand years, and the Order calls them soulless for it.
- **Society:** clutches (extended sibling groups), led by the eldest who still swims. A Kirru who holds on to grief is shamed and, if they refuse to let go, **scraped**: their name-shell is ground blank and they are sent away until they can sing the farewell-tide.
- **Relation to the Order:** tolerated, never converted, and not permitted in temples, with one historical exception: **Ressa**, the only Kirru Cantor (see Pell).
- **The recent change:** since Ressa went into the storm about 70 years ago, Kirru who die near open sea no longer lift cleanly. The Undertow has started pulling at them, through her. The elders won't say it aloud.

## 9. The Undertow (in story terms)

- **Appearance:** a slow, colossal maelstrom-storm, kilometres wide, a wall of dark water and cloud lit from within by glimmer. Its "wounds" are rents where light bleeds out. It moves against the wind.
- **Behaviour:** it wanders between Calms and reaches for the living. Coastal towns drown. Afterward the motes of the drowned hang over the water for days, until an Ebbing herds them out to sea (and back into the storm).
- **Undertow-spawn:** creatures that form from its overflow. They have dark, wet carapaces and drifting glimmer wounds, and they are often grotesque echoes of sea animals. They come ashore ahead of the storm.
- **The voice inside:** anyone who has lost someone to it can, very close, hear voices they know. Rhen hears his father's current-song in Chapter 1 and again at Corrow. It is real: Dace's lamp is inside, still holding together around Imra.
- **Inside (Act III):** layered "rooms" made of the drowned's memories (a market, a kitchen, a Reefball crowd) suspended in dark water lit by motes. At the bottom are the Cage (four bound founders) and the heart (Imra, with Dace beside her).

## 10. The Ending (locked shape from SPEC §3.5)

1. In the heart of the Undertow the party defeats Sefir, who is trying to take the heart into himself and become the storm's eternal heart.
2. Asha sings the Eighth Verse, the Letting. Every mote in Maresh lifts at once: the storm, every ancient lamp, every Saltwright machine, every Vowbound, and every unrisen. This is the **Great Unbinding**. Everything is released in that one instant. What the Ending shows afterwards (the dead still coming up out of the sea, the three goodbyes, the last faint sea-glow) is the *ascent* of light that was already released, and nothing lets go later ([act3_ending §0 rule 7](beats/act3_ending.md)).
3. Imra Velamir appears for one last scene with Asha. Dace Sallow says goodbye to Rhen, and this time Rhen says it back. Kerrid lifts last, because the captain comes off the wall last ([ADR 0020](../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md)).
4. The Undertow ends forever. The Kite's lamps go out and Liss sets it down on the water. Asha can speak, but she will never sing again.
5. **Final image:** Asha on Haluna's dock at night. For the first time in a thousand years the sea is completely dark, and the stars are reflected in it.
6. **Post-credits:** in a rebuilt Oruvel, someone lights the first new oil lamp.

## 11. Canon Notes for Other Roles

- **Terminology:** the spec's working word for the lingering dead is replaced by **unrisen** in all game text, because the original term is too close to existing IP. "The Calm" is kept because the spec uses it, but never build new compounds on it (the CI list in `tools/banned_terms.txt` names the ones that echo existing IP). **Last Calm** (the Order's promise) is the only one in canon, and it stays because it is the Order's own phrase. Sefir's goal is **the Long Stillness**: the sea held still forever with himself as its heart. His earlier working name was a Calm compound and is now on the banned list ([decisions_log N-10](decisions_log.md)).
- **Mote direction** carries meaning (see §2). Rendering and VFX should encode up = released and down/seaward = anchored.
- **The Order hums and Cantors sing.** The choral hymn should be hummed or vocalised by the congregation, with Lamptongue words reserved for Cantor and Choir pieces.
- **Night sea glow** is ambient in every night scene until the ending. Its absence in the final scene is the payoff, so do not cut it from earlier scenes for performance reasons without telling Narrative.
