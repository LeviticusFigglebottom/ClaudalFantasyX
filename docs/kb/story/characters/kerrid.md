# Old Kerrid

**Role:** a silent, scarred wandering swordsman, and secretly one of the **unrisen**. **Apparent age:** 44 (dead for eleven years). **From:** Corrow; formerly the captain of the Corrow **stair-watch**, the conscripted fishers who keep the Fisher Stair.
**Battle niche:** heavy hitter who breaks armour.

## Background
*(Re-staged in [ADR 0020](../../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md) and [ADR 0022](../../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md), with its review-sweep-G append note.)* Kerrid was a Corrow fisher's son, conscripted into the stair-watch at sixteen and its captain for fourteen years. The Stillguard garrison holds the Bastion and the wall-head. The watch holds the stair. He had no rank in the Order, no decoration and no title, and nobody outside Corrow had heard of him.

In 1001 AL, before the Rising, he hired a Low Reaches lamp-diver, **Dace Sallow**, to re-seat the hand-line's iron rings at the foot of the stair below low water. For eight low tides they worked the rings together, Dace under the water at the stair foot and Kerrid on the bottom step holding his line, and Dace slouched. In the same eight days the Cantor **Imra Velamir** came to Corrow for the Coilspire trial with her Order escort. The headland path to Coilspire goes out through the stair-watch's wall-gate, and Kerrid kept the gate: he opened it for her at every dawn and again at every dusk, and they spoke a few words there each time. He never walked the path beside her. She laughed at the wrong times. Dace kept out of her way, because a Saltwright with his marks under his sleeves doesn't sit at a fire with a Cantor's Stillguard. Kerrid was the only man in Corrow who knew them both. They never met.

On the eighth day the Undertow came for Corrow. The sea drew out and came back early, and Dace was under the stair foot at the last ring. Imra, on the wall-head, heard one living lamp still drowning under the stair and went down for him against her escort's orders, a diver she had never met. Kerrid held the Fisher Stair's wall-gate open past the hour the rule allows, went down the stair himself, and got them both onto it. He was the last man on the stair, holding the hand-line for the last climbers, when the sea came over it. The line ran through his hands and burned them to the bone, and he held it. Dace reached back down for him and missed. The sea took Kerrid off the bottom step.

The watch's stair-law is *first down the stair, last up it*: a captain doesn't come off the wall while anyone is still below. Kerrid never came off. His motes would not lift. He woke at the next low tide at the foot of the stair, among the drowned, unrisen. Corrow had already lit his name with the rest of that Rising's drowned. He had twice stood on the wall while the Stillguard performed the Stilling on one of the unrisen, so he knew what the Order would do with him, and he was gone before anyone saw him.

Imra walked on to the Choir and was spent. Dace, who had already lost one man off a stair, went into the storm after her. Kerrid has never been to the Pale Rim, and he doesn't know what became of Dace.

For eleven years he has walked the coasts. Wherever the sea takes someone (a swamped boat, a diver who doesn't surface, a child off a reef), he goes into the water. The Low Reaches tell an anonymous fisher's tale about **a big man in a straw cape** who pulls the drowning out and is gone before anyone can thank him. This year the sea began to come wrong along the coasts, the Undertow stirring before its time (the Rising that takes Oruvel in Ch1), and he started walking toward Corrow, because the storm always breaks at Coilspire in a Rising, and he meant to be at the stair. Asha's naming three years earlier did not move him. He never watched over Asha and never went looking for Rhen. He is now fraying: his memory has gaps, and his wounds leak light.

## Want / Need
- **Wants:** to be at the stair when the sea comes, and to get the last ones up before he comes apart. After Corrow (Ch8), the stair is the pilgrimage: a Cantor's road goes down to the Choir, and somebody is still below.
- **Needs:** to accept that no wall and no stair will ever get everyone up, and to come off the wall. He has to let the watch end, and let himself be finished.

## Arc Beats
- **Act I:** at Pemakar (Ch5) he meets the pilgrimage on his way to Corrow. He sees Imra's face in Asha's, hears the name Sallow, and walks with them because they are going where he is going. He says only, "I held a stair for your mother once," to Asha, and nothing to Rhen. He is silent and capable. Nobody hails him, nobody outside Corrow knows his face, and Toma doesn't connect him with Corrow's dead captain until the Cold Road ([ADR 0017](../../decisions/0017-act-one-restaging.md)).
  - At the Brine Shoals (Ch6) he keeps his back to Sefir's barge. He knows what the Order does with the unrisen, and he doesn't know what a High Warden can see. Sefir doesn't know the face. He sees what Kerrid is.
  - **Clues to plant:** he never eats; he declines inns; his breath never fogs in Ullar later; animals shy from him.
  - At Corrow (Ch8) he works the **foot** of the Fisher Stair, waist-deep in the storm-dark, pushing the last climbers up. An old fishwife on the stair, one of his own watch's, looks back down, stares at him and says "Captain?" He says nothing and goes down a step. Toma and the Stillguard are on the wall-head above, out of earshot. He comes up the stair last, which is why he is there to haul Rhen off the wall's edge by the collar.
  - During the Ebbing (Ch9) he is **alone** at the stair foot, bringing the drowned up out of the low town at slack water (one cutaway). The old watch is on the shore at the Ebbing with the rest of Corrow, so nobody who knows his face sees him there. At dawn he is waiting on the road out. Rhen: "Thought Corrow was where you were going." "It was." "So?" Kerrid looks south, out to sea, not at Asha or anyone on the road: **"Somebody's still below."** "Below" is the sea: Imra under the storm, and the held dead. He is no guard on Asha. The player doesn't yet know this is where he died.
- **Act II:**
  - In Ullar (Ch12) Toma notices the breath. Toma served seven years on the Corrow wall after 1001, where the watch still tells its captain's story. The breath, the rope-burns and the fishwife's word let him put the story to a face.
  - In Ansedra (Ch13–14) he never takes Sefir's hospitality. He sits out the nights at the outer moorings among the barge-polers and never enters the Sanctum. Sefir lets him be: an unrisen is the Long Stillness in one man, and Sefir can wait. At the wedding he stands at the back of the Lacquer Court among the polers. In the escape he holds the water-steps and is last onto Merel's barge.
  - Adrift on the unlamped bridal barge after the wedding (Ch15), the barge passes over a shoal of held dead, who drift toward him as held light drifts toward the sea, and a few of his own motes begin to leave his hands, from the rope-burns. **The reveal is his own confession**, not an exposure: nobody performs a rite on him and nobody attacks. He sits, holds his hands still, and finally speaks at length about Corrow: the Cantor he opened his gate for and the diver whose line he held, a diver drowning under his stair and a Cantor going down for him, the gate held open, the line through his hands, Dace's reach, and the watch he never came off ([ADR 0016](../../decisions/0016-act-two-restaging.md), [ADR 0020](../../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md), [ADR 0022](../../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)).
    - To them both: "She heard a man drowning under my stair and went down for him. A diver she'd never met. Your father, Rhen."
    - To Asha: "I got your mother up the stair. That's all I did. I got her up so she could walk to the Choir."
    - To Rhen: "Your father reached back for me. He missed. I don't think he forgave himself."
    - After this he speaks more freely ("I have nothing left to hide. I may as well talk.").
  - On the Pale Rim (Ch16) he walks last in the line, as on a stair, behind Asha, toward the Choir where the woman he got up his stair was spent. At the Choir he learns from Kesh's memory what he saved her for. He faces Sefir with no shouting and no steel: "I carried her up so you could do this." He doesn't lead the refusal. When the storm wakes he holds the line while the party escapes, first down and last up.
- **Act III:** he is warmer, dryer, and almost funny. He teaches Rhen the last of what Dace taught *him* over eight low tides at the rings. He knows the Unbinding will lift him and that it is the first thing that will ever get everyone up. He leads them into the storm: first down the stair.
- **Ending:** he lifts in the Great Unbinding and is the **last** of the three to go, after Imra and Dace, because the captain comes off the wall last. Everyone was released at H09; he is simply the last of them to rise. When Dace turns in E2 and sees him past Rhen's shoulder, Kerrid shakes his head once: the missed reach was nobody's fault. In E3 he reports the watch's all-clear, "Stair's clear.", and then says a small thing to Rhen: "Stand up straight. Your father slouched."

## Silhouette and Costume
- Tall and heavy, with a hunched-forward weight like a man walking into wind.
- **Silhouette key:** a long, heavy **rain-cape of layered woven palm fibre**, a shaggy cascading form like a haystack on the shoulders, bound across the chest with old rope. It makes him look larger and older than he is.
- **Face:** bare and sea-weathered, with **nothing covered**: broad, with deep squint-lines, a nose broken flat years before he died, grey-black hair cropped short, and a short beard. **Both eyes are clear** and grey.
- **Scars (his death-wounds):** rope-burns spiralling from palm to elbow on both arms, from the stair's hand-line. They never healed. An unrisen's light is held, so in dim light they leak a very faint **`glimmer_held`** cyan, never gold ([ADR 0022](../../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md) (g)). The cue is shown **only from Ch10 on**, never in Ch5 and never in the Ch8 stair-foot shots. At H09 it makes color rule 1's single 1.5 s lerp to `glimmer_lift`, and from then on every mote off him rises gold.
- **Asymmetry:** his **left forearm is wrapped** from knuckle to elbow in tarred hauling-cloth, because that hand took the rope's full run. The right forearm is bare and shows the paler spiral scar. The **stair-watch captain's knotted cord** (three knots, faded watch-red) hangs from the right hip. It is not an Order cord, and he deliberately kept it.
- **Signature weapon:** an **anchor-cleaver**, a massive hooked blade forged from a ship's anchor fluke, carried bare across his back.
- Palette: dry straw, iron, faded watch-red, and bone, with tar-black on the wrapped arm.
- **IP note:** no hat, no coat worn off one arm, no eyewear, no gourd, **no mask or high collar over the face, and no eye effect** ([ADR 0020](../../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md)). He carries no Order insignia and no title ([ADR 0022](../../decisions/0022-dace-imra-strangers-sweep-f.md)). Keep him clear of existing archetypes in this genre.

## Battle Tie-in
Kerrid breaks things: Armor Break, Power Break, and heavy high-rank blows that cost a lot of time but end fights. As one of the dead, he can later learn a skill that ignores the Zombie status and one that **trades his own HP for allies' Regen** ("Spend the Light"). **Suggested Swell input:** a *weighted swing*, a single long charge you release at the right moment, more power the longer you dare.

## Relationships
- **Asha:** the daughter of the woman who went down his stair for a stranger, and whom he got up it so that the Order could spend her.
- **Rhen:** the son of the man whose line he held for eight low tides, who reached back for him. Their relationship is the game's quiet spine.
- **Toma:** a former Stillguard who came to the Corrow wall in 1002, at twenty-one, and served seven years there hearing the watch tell its captain's story. He never saw the man.
- **Sefir:** the head of the Order that would Still him on sight. Sefir never knew his face and didn't kill him. Their confrontation at the Choir is fury with no shouting and no steel, and it is for Imra.
- **Imra and Dace:** the Cantor he opened his gate for and the diver whose line he held. They were strangers to each other until his stair, and they were the last two he got up it. Both are in the storm.
