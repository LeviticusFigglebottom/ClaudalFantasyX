# High Warden Sefir Aurane

**Role:** antagonist. He leads the Order of the Still Water and is the last free Lamplighter. **True age:** about 1,040.
**Combat role:** final boss (multi-phase). See [act outline](../beats/act_outline.md) Ch21.

## Background
In the Lit Age, Sefir was the keeper of Iskarra's Lamp Engines, a brilliant archivist-engineer who loved one person beyond reason: his wife **Kesh**, a singer. When the First Rising drowned Iskarra, he and five other Lamplighters composed the Hush. Kesh sang herself in. Four of them bound themselves as the Cage. Sefir stayed outside because *someone had to keep watch*.

Forty years later the next Cantor displaced Kesh. Sefir couldn't let her lift, so he bound her in the stones of the Choir. In doing so he learned to sustain himself from displaced glimmer, and he has never stopped. He designed the Ebbing to herd the dead seaward, wrote the doctrine of debt, struck the Eighth Verse, and has outlived everyone who ever knew him. He has held the High Warden seat under a dozen names and veils.

He is not cruel by nature. He is someone who could not grieve, and he built a world in that image. **He believes stasis is mercy:** if nothing ends, nothing is lost. He wants the **Long Stillness**: a sea that never stirs again, because he has become the Undertow's eternal heart himself. He would then never die, never be apart from Kesh (whose light he would fold into his), and no one would ever have to lose anyone again, because no one would truly leave.

## Want / Need (as antagonist)
- **Wants:** the Long Stillness, with himself as the eternal heart, Kesh beside him, and the world held still forever.
- **Lack:** he never let Kesh go. Every horror he has done follows from that single refusal. He is what Rhen, Pell, and Ysolde could become.

## Arc Beats
- **Act I:** first appears at the Brine Shoals (Ch6), at the end of the Order raid, on his once-a-Calm progress to see the Salt Edict kept ([ADR 0017](../../decisions/0017-act-one-restaging.md)). His white barge is poled through the smoke and leaves no wake. He stops the brine-branding with one sentence and rebukes his Inquisitor gently. He is warm, witty, and humble. He knelt beside Asha's mother once and says so with real tenderness. He lets Liss go on Toma's papers and looks at her crate a moment too long. His eyes rest on Kerrid for half a beat and move on. He treats Rhen as an amusing young man and not a threat. He is **not** at the Tidecup (Ch7) or the seawall (Ch8).
- **Act II:** hosts the party in Ansedra (Ch13) and gives Asha access to the Sanctum. He gives Liss the run of the Lamp Archive (Ch13) knowing exactly what she will find. He officiates the peace wedding (Ch14), and the trap is sprung by his Conclave while he expresses public sorrow. At the Drowned Choir (Ch16) he tells the truth plainly and reasonably, as if it were the only adult conclusion. His temptation is to go home ([ADR 0016](../../decisions/0016-act-two-restaging.md)): "Leave the Eighth Verse in the stones. Go home to Haluna. Live. No Cantor will ever be named again. I will see to the storm myself." It is the Long Stillness offered as a gift, and it hides its price. The Eighth Verse is the only thing that can undo him, and "I will see to it" means he will take the heart himself. When Ysolde asks who the storm takes then, he smiles and doesn't answer. When Asha refuses, he lets the storm take them, calmly, because he can wait. He always has. He never asks anyone to sing him in. In Ch21 he simply reaches in and takes it.
- **Act III:** he gathers the Abiding and the Cage into the storm's depths to force the change. He is confronted in the heart.
- **Defeat:** in his last phase, Kesh, already released, does not take his hand. He finally sees her lift and he lets her go. Then he lifts too, and his last expression is relief.

## Silhouette and Costume
- Tall and unnaturally still, with perfect posture and almost no idle motion (animators: his breathing should be *absent*).
- **Asymmetry:** a stiff **gold-leaf lacquered half-mantle** over the right shoulder only, worked with tiny overlapping Level symbols like fish scales. The left side is plain pale-blue silk falling straight to the floor.
- **Crown:** a low halo-crown shaped like a shallow **bowl tipped on edge** behind his head, gilded.
- Hair: silver-white, long, worn loose. His face is handsome and ageless.
- **Revenant tells (Art/VFX):** his reflection in water never ripples. His shadow is faintly too sharp. In Act II his eyes catch light like a still pool.
- Palette: gold, ivory, pale still-water blue, and a hint of motes at the fingertips when angered.

## Battle Notes (for Combat)
His phases echo the story: the reasonable Warden (he buffs, delays, and "settles" the timeline by freezing turn order), the Undertow-heart (he draws in the party's Vowbound-adjacent glimmer), and the final stand where Asha's Letting is a countdown the party must protect on the timeline.

## Relationships
- **Kesh:** his wife and his whole reason. Her release ends him.
- **Asha:** his instrument, and in his mind his kindness to her is real.
- **Kerrid:** the Order's dead Hero of Corrow, whose name it lit eleven years ago, and who kept walking. Sefir didn't kill him ([ADR 0020](../../decisions/0020-kerrid-restaging.md)). He knows the face in Ch6 and finds it interesting rather than frightening.
- **Ysolde:** a favoured scholar whose rage surprises him.
- **The Abiding Conclave:** his peers and co-conspirators, whom he would discard to become the heart.
